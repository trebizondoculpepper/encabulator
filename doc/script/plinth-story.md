# The plinth room: recording script

The narrator's lines for dev5, the plinth room (`src/rendering/plinth-room.ts`). Each clip is one recording, played at one moment of the visit. File names are what each recording is saved as (`.wav` or `.flac` from the microphone; they will be compressed for the game).

Where the moment a clip plays is my guess from its content rather than something the owner said, it is marked _(guess)_. Small wording tidies for performance are marked _(tidied)_, with what was said in the note.

## The narrator's feelings

Every line keeps to the narrator's feeling about its shape:

- **The sphere** is good: a trustworthy, dependable shape that reminds us of all that is good and wholesome in the world.
- **The helix** is wistful: regret, lost opportunity, lost love, failure in one's personal life. It is admired, and it hurts.
- **The rhombus** is disgust and suspicion: a shape out of place that stirs opprobrium for reasons never quite stated. Not fear: distaste, and a growing suspicion that it is up to something.

## Triggers

The room already knows, at every arrival: the plinth arrived at, the one visited just before (`previous`), every one before that and how many times this one has been visited (`visit`). Some clips below also need moments the room does not report yet:

- **leaving** a plinth (stepping out of its 4 m with its light going down);
- **lingering**: staying at one plinth for some seconds;
- **near the exit**: coming within a few metres of the way out;
- **in the dark**: being away from all three for some seconds, and longer still.

## Open moments

When two clips fit one moment (a first arrival at the sphere straight from the helix fits clips 1 and 7), the more specific one should win, and the other can wait for a later moment or be skipped. Every moment now has at least one clip. Clip 29 says the door is on the other side of the room: the room's portals are all on its west wall, beside where the visitor arrives, so either an exit goes on the far (east) wall or the line changes.
