# Plan: dev20 component repair workshop

This is the design and follow-up plan for the dev20 repair workshop. The dev20 room has two PLC backplanes, one failed power card, a third true-size 2 A fuse station with a blown cartridge, isolators, fault buttons, a finite replacement-parts bench, status lamps, physical refusal messages, a reset control, and a scrap skip. It implements the shared carry pose, object rotation, coarse collision, isolation interlock, compatible slot placement and magnetic disposal. The broader gameplay requirements remain in `doc/prompts/level0-wire-machine.md`.

## Established patterns and decision

The plan follows four established interaction patterns. [Unreal's Physics Handle](https://dev.epicgames.com/documentation/unreal-engine/BlueprintAPI/Physics/Components/PhysicsHandle?lang=en-US) grabs a physics component at a point, gives it a moving target and releases it. [Unity's XR Grab Interactable](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@2.0/manual/xr-grab-interactable.html) provides an attach point and easing; its documentation explicitly describes the tradeoff between low-latency direct transform movement, which can cross colliders, and velocity-driven movement, which resists that crossing but can lag. [Godot's ShapeCast3D](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_shapecast3d.html) sweeps a whole shape through space rather than a centre ray and reports the safe fraction of motion. [Unity's XR Socket Interactor](https://docs.unity3d.com/Packages/com.unity.xr.interaction.toolkit@3.6/manual/xr-socket-interactor.html) uses a target attach transform, an overlap zone and filters for valid objects.

Adopt those ideas, not a new general physics simulation: one camera-relative hand target, a short damped follow, a swept collision proxy for the carried part, and sockets with explicit attach transforms and compatibility filters. This project uses Three.js without a rigid-body engine, and its existing falling behavior is a small purpose-built model. The first dev20 implementation should use conservative boxes or capsules for the few carried parts and static obstacle volumes, with a shape-sweep helper instead of a full solver. If browser testing shows that this cannot handle the open cabinet and bench geometry without visible clipping or jitter, evaluate a mature physics engine for carried objects before expanding the helper into a home-grown physics system. That is a decision gate, not a dependency in this plan.

## Player experiment

The player sees three circuit boards mounted at comfortable working height on a wall. Each has a labelled, visibly socketed service part and a small local status lamp. One board starts healthy, one starts with a failed part, and one has an empty socket. A clearly marked isolation switch feeds the three boards. Beside them are an electronics waste bin and a workbench with labelled, compatible replacements. The room uses ordinary industrial lighting and physical labels, with no HTML interaction prompts.

The core loop is: observe a failed board; switch its feed off; click the installed part to pick it up; put the failed part in the waste bin; pick up a matching new part from the bench; face the empty socket at close range and click once while holding the part; switch the feed on; press that board's RESET only if its fault was latched; see the board's output and lamp recover. A healthy part can be removed and reseated without becoming a failed part. A failed part remains failed if reseated.

The first specimen should use an existing detachable control-fuse cartridge and holder. The second should use an existing PLC expansion card and its backplane slot, demonstrating the larger board-sized version of the same gesture. The third should use a plausible socketed board component, such as a plug-in relay or period socketed PROM, to test the exact small-component interaction the owner wants. Do not make a soldered electrolytic capacitor magically removable: the present capacitor-board click directly calls `replace()` and is a separate repair model to revise only after this experiment settles the socket interaction.

## Shared pickup and placement behavior to prototype

Pick up into a consistent right-handed carry pose regardless of the object's original distance: its grip point eases over roughly 0.2 seconds to about 0.65 m ahead of the camera, slightly below and right of centre (start with 0.18 m right and 0.18 m down). Keep the object's own orientation and a believable grip offset rather than centring every object on the view. The 0.65 m describes the normal arm pose, not extra reach for a large board. Camera zoom must not change the world-space arm length. Test large cards and small cartridges for legibility and screen obstruction before locking these numbers.

While carrying, sweep the held object's bounds from its last valid pose to the intended pose against three-dimensional solid volumes, including plinths, benches, walls, cabinet shells and closed doors. Let the arm retract toward the camera when a nearer pose is free, then restore its normal length when clear. If no pose within comfortable reach is free, constrain movement or turning at the last safe pose. Never let a carried board pass through a plinth merely because the camera itself clears it. Use a modest collision proxy per part rather than testing every decorative triangle; make open cabinet doors and genuine openings update the solid volumes.

Before ordinary drop physics, try a slot insertion or magnetic bin toss. A held object actioned within 2 m of the nearest edge of a bin or skip's opening is thrown into it, regardless of precise aim; this magnetic range applies to the carried object, not an empty-handed click. If the player drops an object inside an enclosure whose interior has no modeled support outside that toss, place it just outside the closest reachable boundary on the player's side and give it a short outward clatter; compute the escape direction from the camera, not from a fixed world axis. A modeled shelf or bin floor that truly supports the part may hold it instead.

For installation, measure from the carried part's relevant connector or grip anchor to the target socket, not from the camera or the board's centre. The player must bring that anchor within 0.20 m, face a visible compatible empty socket, and use the normal action click. The socket may highlight subtly when this is valid. Then animate the last 0.20 m into the keyed orientation and seat the part; make the electrical connection only when that insertion completes. Require an unobstructed approach and isolation, so a part cannot snap through a closed door, cabinet wall or another part. A click outside the snap zone remains an ordinary release; a rejected attempt near a socket keeps the part in hand and gives a clear local cue. Keep every socket and the replacement bench outside the bin's 2 m magnetic range, with at least 1 m additional clearance for the player's carry pose and approach. The layout, rather than an invisible exception to the magnetic rule, prevents an installation click from becoming a throw.

The current prototype uses shared object handling for the carry pose and a pre-drop room action for insertion and disposal. Collision volumes are currently supplied by dev20; other rooms need their own coarse solids. `ejectCabinetDrop` still pushes toward a fixed cabinet front, and player-side ejection remains follow-up work.

## Interaction rules

| Action                                                                                            | Result                                                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Click a service part while its board is live                                                      | Refuse removal; leave it connected and give a short local mechanical refusal cue.                                                        |
| Isolate, then click the installed part                                                            | Disconnect its function immediately and carry the actual part object using the shared pickup controls. The empty socket remains visible. |
| Action a held part within 2 m of the waste bin's opening edge                                     | Throw it into the bin without requiring precise aim; the part remains discarded and cannot satisfy a socket.                             |
| Click while holding a compatible part, with its connector within 0.20 m of a visible empty socket | Snap it into place with a brief insertion motion and contact click; connect its model when seated. No new key or gesture.                |
| Click near a socket with an incompatible part, at an occupied socket, or while live               | Refuse insertion and keep the part in hand, with clear physical feedback. No silent repair.                                              |
| Re-energize after a healthy part is seated                                                        | Restore output automatically where appropriate; require the board's own RESET where its failure is latched.                              |

The room's release diversion should recognize a specific socket hit and compatible carried part before bin tossing and ordinary drop physics. Match by part kind and socket identity, not by proximity alone. An insertion should hand ownership back to the board and call the existing portable component's reset or installation transition once. The room should keep a registry of installed, held, loose, and discarded instances so that a thrown-away failed part cannot reappear on the bench or count as repaired.

## Test controls and feedback

Give each specimen a physical ISOLATE switch, TEST/FAIL control for repeatable fault injection, status lamp, and a small output indicator that proves the part's effect. The initial room loads one fault so the loop is visible immediately. Keep the fault injection control separate from RESET: RESET may clear a latch after the cause is fixed, but it must never restore a blown, missing, or discarded part. The bench should have a finite, visible stock of labelled replacements, with a room RESET that restores the experiment for another attempt. Do not auto-restock during a repair, because scarcity and provenance are part of this test.

Use a small electronics waste bin rather than a full skip in this workshop, reusing the existing bin tossing path but extending its magnetic action range to 2 m from the opening edge. Put it across the room from the wall boards and spare-parts bench; no socket or point where the player must carry and place a replacement may enter that range, including the part's 0.65 m carry offset. Mark a simple working zone in front of the boards and a separate disposal zone around the bin. Give discarded parts a distinct drop sound appropriate to their size. Use the same first-person click/pickup/release controls as the rest of the game. The board and socket labels must remain readable from working distance, and the installed part must not block the click target of an adjacent part.

## What to test in dev20

1. Isolate the failed PLC backplane, remove its power card, discard it in the skip, collect its matching spare, aim at its open slot and use to align and insert it, and restore power. The board lamp should turn green.
2. At the fuse station, turn on the isolator to see the fault lamp and live-removal refusal. Isolate, extract the blown cartridge, discard it, collect the new 2 A cartridge from the bench, aim at the empty holder and use to insert it, then re-energize. The fuse lamp should turn green.
3. Try a wrong card, a card from the other board, an occupied socket, and a live socket. The part should stay in hand and a physical message should explain the refusal. Rotate held parts with X and the mouse, touch INSPECT, or gamepad Y.
4. Use RESET EXPERIMENT to reload a fresh finite set of parts and repeat the sequence. The shared carry control and player-side ejection for other rooms remain separate work before Level 0 adopts this mechanic.

## Acceptance for the prototype

The player can finish the failed specimen without a debug command or a hidden health mutation. The replacement is a distinct healthy physical instance from the bench. The old component stays in the bin. The board works again only after a compatible installed part, restored power, and any required local reset. Pickup settles into the same right-handed arm pose from any initial distance; carried parts do not cross solids; unintended drops leave unmodeled enclosure volumes toward the player; only a compatible part brought within 0.20 m of a reachable socket snaps in; and an action within 2 m of the bin throws the held part in. The workbench and sockets remain safely outside that magnetic range during the whole installation approach. Attempted shortcuts fail visibly and leave the machine's true state intact. The entire loop can be repeated in the dev room before Level 0 is changed.
