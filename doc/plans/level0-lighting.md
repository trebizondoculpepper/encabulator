# Plan: Level 0's lighting comes to life

Agreed with the owner on 19 October 2026. The theme of the game is machines coming to life, and in Level 0 that is to include the building's lighting: lights that stutter on as if waking, notice the player, and answer to the machine (blinking as its motors start, browning out when it is run hard for long, going out in its catastrophes), with emergency lighting when they fail. Everything is tried first in dev18 (Building Fittings), then fitted to Level 0's warehouse. Models are placeholders at true size after real products (the owner will have another agent improve them); the effort goes into behaviour. Research: `doc/research/emergency-lighting-parts.md` and `doc/research/warehouse-lighting-control.md`.

Already built in dev18 (uncommitted at the time of writing, 19 October): the supply's quality (normal, brownout, severe), LED drivers, per-fitting emergency changeover, emergency downlights, door bulkheads and a maintained exit sign checked against BS 5266-1, a LIGHTING FAULT trip, three lighting zones on one grid switch bank with the emergency test key, and a test key that cuts only the emergency fittings' supply.

The work is briefed in numbered prompts, in order: `doc/prompts/001-lighting-zones-and-emergency-lighting.md` (done), `002-fluorescent-battens-come-to-life.md`, `003-level0-darker-day-and-zoned-lighting.md`, `004-level0-machine-load-drives-the-lights.md`, `005-level0-catastrophes-and-strange-lights.md` and `006-better-models-for-lighting-fittings.md`.

## Decisions

- **Direct switching, as the research recommends** (owner, 19 October): no contactors. Each zone is switched by grid switches and its presence detector; the board holds one breaker per zone.
- **A switch bank at both exits** (owner): every zone two-way switched from a bank by each personnel door, as a long corridor is; the emergency test key on one of them.
- **Each zone wired as its own circuit** (owner): LIGHTING ZONE 1, 2 and 3 breakers on the distribution board, so a fault can take out one row.
- **Fluorescent, not LED, with switch-start control gear** (recommended; the owner asked for "realistic" switching on "with fluorescent ballast noises" and chose switch-start on 19 October): a 1990s trading-estate unit plausibly still has twin 5 ft T8 battens on switch-start gear, which come on with the starter's glow and click, the tube flickering, catching and failing, then striking and settling to the ballast's hum. Electronic gear (a near-clean start) remains an option behind the same model.

## 1. Zones, switching and detectors (dev18, then shared)

- **Model** (`src/toolkit/components/architectural/logic/building-services.ts`): replace the three `lights.zoneN` inputs with, per zone, two two-way switches (one at each bank; the zone is on when they differ, as the office's two-way pair does), a breaker of its own (`board.zoneN`, tripping each on its own), and a presence detector's state. The zone's supply is main switch × its breaker; its demand is its two-way pair × its detector. The emergency fittings of a zone are fed from that zone's circuit (unswitched), so a zone breaker tripping lights that zone's emergency fittings only, and the main switch lights them all. Actions: `tripZone(n)`, and the severe disturbance trips every zone. Tests: two-way from either bank, one zone's trip leaves the others, emergency fittings follow their zone's circuit, the test key still cuts only the emergency supply.
- **Presence detection** (new `src/toolkit/components/architectural/logic/presence-detector.ts`, pure, tested): after CP Electronics' EBDHS-PRM (research Part 5). A detector sees the player within its circle (an 18 m diameter at 7 m, per the research's estimate) and holds its zone on for its time-out after last seeing them; switched off by time-out, the zone comes back on when the player walks under it (absence/presence configurable). A room feeds it the player's position each frame. Time-out: 15 minutes as installed, but Level 0 can shorten it so the lights noticeably follow the player.
- **Parts and views**: `gridSwitchPlate` (built) takes two-way bindings; a second bank at the other exit; a `presenceDetector` placeholder (88 mm white disc with a faceted dome, a red walk-test LED as licence) per zone on the roof steel; the distribution board's circuits gain LIGHTING ZONE 1/2/3 (the board view already takes a list of circuits).
- **dev18**: banks by the escape door and by the utility door (the room's two personnel exits), detectors over each column, the board's three zone breakers, the LIGHTING FAULT button replaced by a FAULT ZONE selector or one fault button per zone on the test plate.

## 2. Fluorescent battens that come to life (dev18)

- **Research first** (small): a common UK twin 5 ft T8 58 W switch-start batten of the 1990s (Thorn PopPack, or an IP65 non-corrosive one after Thorn Aquaforce), its size, its gear (wire-wound ballast, glow starter, power-factor capacitor), its run-up behaviour and time, its hum, and what a failing tube does (end-blackening, flickering, a starter that keeps trying). Append to `doc/research/emergency-lighting-parts.md` or a new `doc/research/fluorescent-battens.md`.
- **Logic** (new `src/toolkit/components/architectural/logic/fluorescent-gear.ts`, pure, tested; replaces `led-driver.ts` for these fittings, which stays for LED ones): per tube, with a seed giving it its own age and quirks. Switched on: the starter glows for 0.3 to 1 s, opens (a click) and the tube tries to strike; it may fail and retry, each failed strike a flash and a tink; it strikes after 0.5 to 3 s for a healthy tube, longer for an old one; it runs up from a dim, slightly pink glow to full in about a second. Once lit it holds through dips down to about 80 % of nominal and drops out below that, restriking through the same flicker when the supply returns (the research: an arc goes unstable below about 80 %). An old tube may never catch: it blinks, its starter clicking, until switched off and on. Events out (`glow`, `click`, `flash`, `strike`, `out`) for sounds and lamp; output 0 to 1 for light. Tests: strike times within range and different between tubes, retries for old tubes, drop-out below 80 % and restrike, a dud blinks until reset, deterministic for a seed, any frame rate.
- **Sound** (placeholders, positional, each from its fitting through the part audio): the starter's click, the strike's tink and ping, a faint flicker crackle, and the ballast's 100 Hz hum held while lit, as a dev12 machine tone (synthesised, seamless, running up as the tube strikes). Recordings may replace the synthesised ones later, credited in `CREDITS.md`. The hum of a dozen fittings is kept quiet and positional, not a wall of noise.
- **Light**: the batten's lens and its light follow the tube's output, so a zone switched on ripples on unevenly, tube by tube; in a brownout tubes drop out and restrike all over the room.
- **Part and view**: a `fluorescentBatten` part (placeholder: twin 5 ft batten, 1.5 m, two tubes in a white or IP65 grey body), built on the existing luminaire part, with `gear: 'switch-start' | 'electronic' | 'led'`.

## 3. Level 0

- **Darker day**: daylight from 0.5 to about 0.2 (`level0-scene.ts`), the battens a little brighter, the bounce light following the battens as in dev18, so the room is lit mostly by its own lights and goes properly dark without them.
- **The warehouse's lighting** (`src/scene/warehouse-training-room.ts`, shared with dev17 and Level 2): its twelve luminaires grouped into three zones of four (the rows the research recommends); a switch bank at the south personnel door and one at the north escape door in place of the domestic switches; a detector per zone; zone breakers in its distribution board; emergency downlights laid out by Thorn's guide for 7.2 m and bulkheads over its exits, checked by `checkEmergencyLighting` in a test of the warehouse plan (as dev18's is); exit signs. The point lights that light the room follow their zone's fittings (one light per fitting, or per pair, kept within the draw budget). dev17 and Level 2 get the fittings too (they share the shell) but keep their own supply steady.
- **The machine's load drives the supply** (`src/game/level0-plant.ts` and a bridge in `level0-scene.ts`): the lighting's voltage is read from the plant rather than the supply selector: a dip each time a big motor starts (its inrush, from the starters the plant already models: a fraction of a second, deep enough to blink some tubes), and a brownout when the machine runs over capacity for long (overcapacity: the plant's load current above a threshold of the main disconnect's 100 A rating, as with the fan on BOOST and the drive near full speed or worn parts drawing more; sustained for a few minutes it sags the building's supply and makes it wander: tubes flicker, drop out and restrike until the load comes down). Tests in the plant's tests: a motor start dips; overcapacity sustained browns out and recovers.
- **Catastrophes**: KAPUT's bangs and a fire surge the lights (a brief swell, then a severe disturbance), and trip lighting zones; the room drops to emergency lighting and the player resets the zone breakers at the board, the tubes stuttering back to life zone by zone.
- **Strangeness**: as strain rises, the lights join the strangeness (`src/game/level0-strangeness.ts`): a tube here and there flickers, then more, the ballast hum rising and falling with the machine, all stopping at breakdown, as the other effects do.
- **The scenario simulator**: a SYMPTOMS switch for a brownout and a FAULTS switch for a lighting trip, as the other faults have.
- **Budget**: Level 0's draw budget test (`src/rendering/level0-draw-budget.test.ts`) still passes; lamps are live (not merged), the emergency lights' spotlights are counted, and the number of real lights in the shader is kept small (one per zone's pair of fittings, if one per fitting is too many).
