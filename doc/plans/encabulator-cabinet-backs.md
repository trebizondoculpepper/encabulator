## Proposed appearance

The back should read as seven cabinets bolted together: powder-blue steel panels, narrow dark seams, repeated fasteners and a continuous plinth. Keep most large surfaces quiet. Concentrate the visual detail in the two service sections, cable-entry areas and the drive's ventilation. Preserve the current footprint and height, current branding system and deliberately tidy industrial appearance.

Number sections by their existing front-view order. Walking around to the back reverses their apparent order: the rear elevation reads **7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1** from left to right. Do not renumber components or use warehouse compass directions as machine-local directions.

Fixed panels carry screw heads rather than handles that suggest an unavailable interaction.

## Geometry and routing constraints

Keep the local rear plane at z = −0.508 m and reuse existing section widths. The existing continuous rear sheet must be replaced section by section; do not leave it behind new doors. Give panels a folded-edge impression, shallow recesses, gasket lines and source-based sheet thickness. Use existing worn paint, with restrained handling marks and dirt at seams, not generalized rust.

The approximately 133 mm zone between the rear plane and current bucket liner is not empty: existing buses occupy some of it. Make a measured cross-section before routing. Reserve the existing upper horizontal bus route and each section's vertical bus strip behind fixed opaque guards. Shape service openings around these guards; neither an open rear door nor an opened front bucket should expose otherwise unguarded copper. Do not move busbars or deepen the machine casually to make the dressing fit.

Mount rear duct on brackets within the remaining service volume. Use the researched 60 × 40 mm duct cross-section where it fits; choose and cite a smaller catalogue size if required. Route along the section edges with closed lids and short branches to actual endpoints. Power and signal routes should occupy distinct channels. Enclosed transitions through section partitions provide inter-cabinet connections; a visible loom must not pass through solid steel.

Use a small explicit route schedule: endpoint identity, cable purpose, diameter, route points, gland/grommet, support points and visibility group. Reuse existing terminal/device identities. Treat these routes as appearance metadata, not a second electrical simulation. If an endpoint is not represented in the current equipment view, add the appropriate connector to that same component view rather than a floating duplicate.

At the base, preserve the existing isolator-to-floor-to-machine trunking. Add a credible termination into section 1's entry plate and covered internal continuation. Do not invent cables going to external loads that the room has not modeled. Use blanked glands or labeled covered terminal routes instead. Give moving doors only the wiring they actually carry, with service slack and an anchored bonding strap; otherwise keep bonding fixed to the chassis.

## Implementation stages

1. **Measure and map.** Re-read the current builder, machine wrappers, component views and gallery rules. Record section/bucket/device transforms and the bus, tray and door swept volumes. The code is evolving; use the current implementation rather than assuming research-time line numbers. Produce one rear elevation and a cross-section in the completion report before detailed dressing.
2. **Build reusable fittings.** Add source-based rear-panel/door geometry, gland/blanking plate, duct with lid, cable supports, bonding connection and fan/filter views where existing equivalents are insufficient. Machine enclosure and internal panel fittings belong under `industrial-control`; building-installed equivalents belong under `architectural`. Put reusable fittings in the appropriate existing gallery, with labels and meaningful interactions only. Passive metalwork does not need fake buttons or independent power state.
3. **Replace the outer back.** Add per-section rear specifications and a machine-specific assembly module, proposed `src/scene/encabulator-rear.ts`. Retain the authoritative front equipment and component instances. Install section framing, fixed covers, guards and rear doors with stable names. Route dressing through the measured service zones without altering established supply or board connections.
4. **Integrate shared access behaviour.** Reuse `doorLatch`/`doorBack`, their sounds and the machine's animation update. Rear fasteners open/close their own leaf; an open leaf also closes from its back surface. Keep front/rear state separate. Preserve the existing front-door API, index ordering, scenario bindings and `doorCount`; introduce a distinct rear-access API or metadata for development rather than appending rear doors to the old indexed front list.
5. **Correct visibility and picking.** Replace front-only culling for affected content with front-access OR rear-access OR existing always-render policy, including the period while either leaf is still swinging. Finer rear-only wiring groups can use the rear door's visibility alone. Keep guard/backplate geometry opaque, so enabling a group does not permit clicking through its solid back. Continue ticking all component logic when hidden. Preserve the PLC window's existing always-render exception and fixed motherboard attachment.
6. **Integrate and verify in all scenes.** Shared appearance should reach dev6, Level 0 and Level 2 through the same machine assembly. Inspect machine/walk collision margins and rear-door swings in each placement, updating shared bounds only if new fixed protrusions require it. Keep the existing interaction reach and universal inputs. Do not wire a rear opening to Level 2's front-automation-door narration cue. Record gallery additions, performance and inspection views.

## Verification and acceptance

- Geometry: seven correctly sized rear sections, five mainly hard-backed sections and two service sections with three rear leaves; no remaining continuous sheet over an opening, overlapping panels, floating cable endpoints or unblanked unused entries. No duplicate functional components.
- Interaction: all new leaves start closed, each opens by its own fasteners and closes by either fastener or its rear surface. Opening rear access never changes a front-door state. Front-door indices and existing control interactions remain unchanged.
- Visibility: test front closed/rear closed, front open/rear closed, front closed/rear open and both open, including closing animations. Visible-through-window components remain rendered. Solid liners/guards prevent access through metal; a hidden interior cannot be picked. Culling never changes model state or board presence.
- Machine behaviour: exercise startup/shutdown, isolation, a board removal/reseat and a fault/recovery. Check Level 2's existing front-door narrative trigger and skip behaviour. Rear access must not add bypasses to removal guards or make soldered components detachable.
- Appearance: capture rear overall, section 4 open, both section 5 leaves open, drive ventilation, base entry and both oblique ends, in the darker Level 0 lighting and dev6. Check both floor clearance and clearance to existing room props.
- Performance: compare draw calls/triangles before and after with rear access closed/open from equivalent views; use shared materials and merged static detail where safe. Do not merge moving leaves or clickable fittings. Decide any necessary simplifications from measurements.
- Validation: add meaningful geometry/raycast and visibility integration tests beside the existing MCC tests. Run typecheck, lint, formatting check, tests and build before any requested commit.

## Deliberate limits and remaining design decisions

This plan chooses accessible service doors, not permanently missing rear covers. It models connection appearance without adding a new wiring/fault system, changing room layout or converting the machine to a certified UK switchboard. Exact connector families and cable diameters should be selected against the actual component terminals during stage 1; they are not established by the general enclosure references. The fan is a visual reference pending clearance checks, not a calculated cooling solution. If the measured rear volume cannot support a proposed opening, reduce the opening or expose the genuine backplate and edge wiring; do not delete a necessary mounting plate to reveal a more dramatic but impossible PCB view.
