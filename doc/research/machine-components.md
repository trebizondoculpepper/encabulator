# Machine components: physical references

The dev0 gallery is 20 × 16 m, with eighteen separate 900 mm square plinths in three rows on 2 m × 3 m centres. Devices retain their metre dimensions; the sloping test panels use ordinary unscaled parts. `MACHINE_REFERENCES` supplies the product names printed on the plinths and the source attached to every shared device. dev6 instantiates the same `machineComponent` factory.

These are simplified game models of real products, not engineering reproductions or electrical safety simulations. Enclosures, DIN rails, cable ducts, wires and mounting hardware are passive scene geometry. Equipment that switches, disconnects, fails or is replaced is a part with component state and its own actions. Electronic conversion and PLC operation are deliberately simplified; the game does not emulate firmware, ladder logic or transistor switching.

The gallery supplies each device independently and exposes load/input, fault injection, repair/replacement and nonpressable ACTIVE and FAULT/OUT lamps. In the MCC the starter's control feed depends on its breaker, fuse and terminal connection; overload trip drops the contactor. PLC supply and processor availability gate the output modules. Withdrawal and faults interrupt operation. Closed bucket interiors are hidden and excluded from use raycasts while their state continues to advance.

Trial custom PCB backplanes use the shared `createPcbBackplane` view: 480 × 420 mm for the PLC assembly and 300 × 450 mm for the drive, both 2.4 mm thick on standoffs. Their procedural artwork is decorative, adapted from [ArtBIT Circuit Board Painter](https://github.com/ArtBIT/circuitboard-painter) under MIT; it does not reproduce the manufacturer's copper layout. The same trial boards appear in the processor and power-stage gallery exhibits. Explicit `?room=dev6&view=plc&doors=open` is available for inspection; ordinary starts keep every door closed.

The PCB BACKPLANE REV C is now a complete `backplane` part, shared by the MCC and its own eighteenth gallery plinth (`?room=dev0&view=backplane`). It owns five separate serviceable expansion cards spanning six slot pitches: supply, two-slot processor, digital input, digital output and analogue monitoring. Their in-world names are Betaform Amplifier, Modial Interaction Controller, Plynost Modulation Rectifier, Hydrocoptic Phase Regulator and Baltovsky Flux Compensator. The separate communications-card gallery exhibit remains available but is not fitted to this five-card assembly. Each card has its own connector and removable state; the card PCB, edge contacts and chip packages are exposed during withdrawal. Bus failure or disconnect cuts all card power; removing the supply cuts downstream power and removing the processor inhibits the output card. The board artwork and assembly are custom, based on the already documented 1771 card format.

The shared Use action now picks up removable Pepperdyne cards and boards in dev0/dev6, holds them at their original pickup distance while walking/looking, and drops them on the next use. Loose boards stay disconnected and settle on the floor, plinths or cabinet shelves with dust and a synthesised clatter; they can be picked up again. Pepperdyne is the fictional in-world supplier for the planned training joke; the listed real products remain modelling references. The narrator reveal, smoking-machine chapter ending and waste-bin disposal sequence are not yet scripted.
