# What a 1990s American plant needed to be up to code

The hall is set in a large American plant in the late 1990s. Three bodies of rules applied to a building like it then; this note lists what each asks for that a player would see, and what the hall has or lacks. Figures come from the sources listed; where a figure could not be confirmed for a 1990s edition, it says so.

## Which codes applied

- **Federal workplace rules: OSHA, 29 CFR 1910 (General Industry).** These applied everywhere, whatever the local building code: sanitation, first aid, exits, fire extinguishers, powered industrial trucks, electrical clearances, hazard communication. ([eCFR: 1910.141](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-29/subtitle-B/chapter-XVII/part-1910/subpart-J/section-1910.141), [OSHA: 1910.157](https://www.osha.gov/laws-regs/regulations/standardnumber/1910/1910.157))
- **A regional model building code.** Before the International Building Code (first widely adopted in 2000), a state or city adopted one of three: the BOCA National Building Code in the East and Midwest, the Standard Building Code (SBCCI) in the South, or the Uniform Building Code (ICBO) in the West, each with a matching plumbing code. ([PHCP Pros: history of US model codes](https://www.phcppros.com/articles/22794-the-history-of-us-model-code-development), [AWC: the IBC and IRC](https://awc.org/publications/the-international-building-code-and-its-impact-on-wood-frame-design-and-construction))
- **NFPA standards, referenced by both.** NFPA 101 Life Safety Code (exits), NFPA 13 (sprinklers), NFPA 30 (flammable liquids), NFPA 70 National Electrical Code, NFPA 72 (fire alarms).
- **The Americans with Disabilities Act (1990) and its 1991 Accessibility Guidelines**, for new and altered buildings: accessible restrooms and routes, visible alarms.

## Suggested additions, roughly in order of how they would read

1. Fire extinguishers on the columns and walls, each under a red sign, about every 75 ft.
2. Exit doors in the side walls, with EXIT signs and emergency light packs, and pull stations and horn-strobes at every exit.
3. A forklift battery charging station: chargers on the wall, a bunded area, its own eyewash, NO SMOKING signs.
4. A break room off the office block: tables, chairs, vending machines, a microwave, a refrigerator.
5. Locker rooms, if the work needs protective clothing.
6. Yellow flammables cabinets and an MSDS station by the chemical store.
7. The sprinkler riser with its alarm valve and gauges in a corner, and a fire department connection on an end wall.
8. Midrails, toeboards and stairs for the balconies.
9. A safety notice board by the time clock with the OSHA poster.

## Done (October 2026)

All nine suggested additions are now in the hall (`src/scene/code-compliance.ts`, placed in `src/rendering/hall-room.ts`): extinguishers on every nave column, on the long walls and by the end doors; five exit doors in the side walls (`NORTH_EXITS`, `SOUTH_EXITS` in `src/scene/industrial-hall.ts`), each with an EXIT sign, an emergency light pack, a pull station and a horn-strobe, as the end walls' personnel doors also now have; a forklift battery charging station with its eyewash; a break room with lockers and benches; flammables cabinets and an MSDS station; separate oxygen and fuel gas cages 6.5 m apart; a stair with guardrails and midrails up to the north balcony; and a safety notice board by the time clock. The balconies' 1.2 m concrete upstand already serves as their guard.

The sprinkler riser and overhead sprinkler pipework were removed from the development Hall on 2026-10-15 to reduce rendering load. The earlier checklist above records the original design research, not the current installed equipment.
