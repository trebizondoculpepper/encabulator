# Module 2: Standard Encabulation Operation

_First-person interactive sequence. Draft in progress. Terminology draws on VX lore and the Encabulator; the operating sequence and nominal setpoints are game fiction. There are five removable Pepperdyne expansion cards, each with a different function and name. All belong to the modial-interaction board family; the betaform amplifier is one of them._

## Expansion cards

The backplane contains exactly five Pepperdyne expansion cards. The suggested removal order below gives each card a distinct identity. Names are provisional and match the model's face and PCB markings.

| Card / slot | Name                          | Role in the component model  |
| ----------- | ----------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| 1           | Betaform Amplifier            | Supply / amplification stage |
| 2           | Modial Interaction Controller | Processor / control logic    |
| 3           | Plynost Modulation Rectifier  | Input conditioning           |
| 4           | Hydrocoptic Phase Regulator   | Output control               |
| 5           | Baltovsky Flux Compensator    | Analogue monitoring          |

**ORDER:** Names belong to the physical cards, not the order of pickup. If the player chooses a different order, identify the card they actually pulled; first/second/third-card reactions still follow the number removed. The timed and emotional reactions do not require a particular card identity.

## Interior — daytime — Training Room 2 — Neumann Dynamik, a German electronics firm, offices on the Guildford Trading Estate, England

**SETTING:** THE ENCABULATOR stands five metres from the middle of the north wall of a mostly empty, 30 × 30 m warehouse training room on the Guildford Trading Estate. A steel incoming-isolator enclosure stands to the left of the machine, connected to the floor and MAIN INCOMING by thick galvanised trunking. Both the incoming isolator and the machine's MAIN DISCONNECT are initially OFF. There is no bin anywhere in the room; the far end is about ten seconds' walk from the machine. Its cabinet doors are closed.

**VIEW:** The player sees through their own eyes throughout. Actions happen in the room, using the machine's physical controls.

**CAPTION OVERLAY:** “Module 2: Standard Encabulation Operation”

**MUSIC:** An optimistic training-film melody or jingle plays. The caption fades away.

**REMINDER PACING:** Give the player at least twenty seconds of quiet after narration finishes before the first sarcastic reminder or any further unsolicited reminder. Time spent speaking or waiting for queued dialogue does not count. Action-triggered reactions remain immediate. Floor reminders wait until their due time and this quiet interval are both satisfied.

## Prime the oil pump — first fault

**PLAYER ACTION:** Turn the LUBE OIL PUMP M-201 operating handle to ON, after completing both normal-operation adjustments.

**MACHINE / SOUND:** The machine conks out. Its mechanisms falter and stop, the hum dies away, and it remains stalled. This first fault produces no smoke or burnt components. It does not recover on its own.

**TRIGGER:** Once the machine has stopped:

**NARRATOR:** Hmm. That's the right switch. Try switching the oil pump off, and then on again. The LUBE OIL PUMP handle. Off first. Then on.

**PLAYER ACTION:** Switch the LUBE OIL PUMP handle OFF. Then switch it ON again. Both operations are required; waiting, or leaving it on, does not restart the machine.

**MACHINE / SOUND:** The machine reboots: lamps flicker and its mechanisms cough, catch and run up again. The oil panel lights and a distinctly liquid “blub, blub, blub” joins the hum. Wait until the restart finishes before the reliability speech.

**NARRATOR:** There. Back to normal. Neumann Dynamik equipment is designed for absolute reliability. Occasionally it simply needs reminding. That is why we have a procedure. Follow the procedure, and you can rely on the machine.
