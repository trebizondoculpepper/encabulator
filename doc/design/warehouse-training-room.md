# Guildford warehouse training room

An additional architectural shell, reached at `?room=dev17` and linked between dev16 and Rhombus. The original office training room is preserved. The same room builder is now instantiated by Level 2 (dev18, the architectural fittings gallery, is now a plain box room of its own); dev17 itself remains empty of scenario apparatus, machines, furniture and loose props. Each instance has its own fittings and state.

The interior is 30 × 30 m, with a 7.2 m roof line. Exposed perimeter columns and thirty-metre lattice girders at six-metre centres keep the central floor clear. Purlins, roof-plane bracing, profiled metal decking and ten framed translucent rooflights explain the construction. The roof is flat in appearance; drainage falls are a design assumption rather than an exposed pitched roof.

The floor is natural-grey power-floated concrete with a restrained satin sheen, broad finishing variations, fine pores and trowel marks. Six-metre slab divisions have narrow sealed saw cuts, with a handful of small repairs and light wheel rubs near the loading entrance. The concrete is treated to prevent dusting and remains clean. Lower walls have a grey protective paint band and warm off-white painted masonry above it. Pale grey profiled metal forms the upper walls. The roof lining is off-white; structural steel is light grey. Hardware and the folding loading door use dull, subtly mottled galvanised metal. Utility doors are muted grey-blue painted steel.

The owner defines the large gate as SOUTH (+Z) and the opposite fire escape as NORTH (-Z). A closed 6 × 5 m loading door with four broad hinged leaves, track, hinges and locking rods occupies the south wall. Its visual reference is [HAG's horizontal folding industrial door](https://www.hag.co.uk/product/c-door/); this is a custom architectural opening, not a claim to reproduce an exact product configuration. The south pedestrian door and opposing north fire exit are the development-room links. Closed west-wall doors identify toilets, accessible WC, first aid and building services. Their rooms are deliberately not modelled.

The retained industrial fabric has modern lighting, clear escape approaches, emergency lights, running-man exit signs, call points, sounder-beacons, fire-action notices and extinguishers. Electrical distribution, British sockets, rigid conduit, cable ladders, heaters, vents and detectors sit around the edges or overhead. Safety equipment reuses the existing catalogue-sized UK fitting views. The eventual training occupancy, equipment and fire strategy will determine any further requirements; this empty game shell does not constitute a certified building design.

Surface references: [Sika warehouse concrete flooring](https://gbr.sika.com/en/construction/flooring/industrial-flooring/warehousing-and-storage/cementious.html) and [clear concrete dustproofing](https://gbr.sika.com/en/distribution/landscaping/surface-cleanerstreatments/sika-dustprooferconcretehardener.html). All texture artwork is generated locally from deterministic seeds; no manufacturer photographs are distributed. Fire/access references consulted during discussion were [Approved Document B](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-approved-document-b), [warehouse fire-risk guidance](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-risk-assessment-factories-and-warehouses) and [Approved Document M](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/access-to-and-use-of-buildings-approved-document-m).

## Shared room and architectural components

`createWarehouseTrainingRoom(kit, gallery)` constructs the common shell, fixed architectural parts, collision footprints and luminaire positions. Callers instantiate it rather than copying its meshes. `addWarehouseLighting` supplies shared daylight and luminaire lighting. The gallery flag adds identification labels and inspection specimens. Dev17, dev18 and Level 2 use the same geometry; Level 2 alone adds scenario apparatus and places its machine five metres from the north wall. Its skip candidates lie approximately twenty metres south of the machine, outside the gate's keep-clear apron.

Every installed fitting is registered in `src/toolkit/components/architectural/logic/architectural-fittings.ts`, with a physical reference and source, and constructed through `architecturalPart`. The distribution board uses the Hager Invicta JK104B 465 × 500 × 132.5 mm enclosure with a hollow case, opening cover and enclosed breakers. The twin socket uses MK Metalclad Plus proportions and BS 1363 slots, with optional plug-in testers for gallery demonstrations. The full architectural catalogue is exhibited in dev18; the board, socket and lighting switch are additionally displayed in dev0. No fitting is scaled to fit an exhibit.

The sprinkler installation currently models its appearance: a three-metre pendant grid, coupled steel branches, header, riser, valve assembly, alarm-test valve and bell. Alarm call points, sounders, detector heads, exit signs and water/CO₂ extinguishers reuse the existing shared building parts. At the owner's request, room-wide fire/electrical service wiring is deferred. Existing component controls and independent states remain available for later binding; an alarm button does not open sprinkler heads.

Industrial door furniture is shared through `src/toolkit/components/architectural/views/door-hardware.ts`: rounded 19 mm tubular return-to-door levers, 300 mm-centre pull handles and a rim-latch panic device with a tubular bar and PUSH BAR TO OPEN legend. Sources: [ARRONE pull furniture](https://www.hoppe.com/in-en/product/1001705277/ar9216-pull-handles), [Briton 378M](https://briton.co.uk/en/products/briton-378.html), [Thorn Aquaforce Pro](https://www.thornlighting.co.nz/en-nz/products/indoor-lighting/industrial-luminaires/Aquaforce_Pro), [Legrand Swifts cable ladder](https://www.legrand.co.uk/en/catalog/swifts-cable-ladder-pre-galvanised), and the individual source links in the architectural catalogue. Some inherited safety fittings reproduce a product family rather than an exact certified assembly; reference links do not imply certification of this game model.

## Floor safety markings

The floor is marked as a tidy UK workplace marks it (`src/toolkit/components/general-safety/views/floor-safety-markings.ts`), in BS ISO 3864 colours: red and white hatched keep-clear zones, 2 × 2 m, in front of both escape doors (FIRE EXIT KEEP CLEAR at the fire exit, KEEP CLEAR at the entrance) and 1 × 0.8 m under each fire point's extinguishers, because escape routes and firefighting equipment must be kept clear; a walkway in solid yellow 50 mm floor tape, 1.6 m wide, along the side between the two escape doors, clear of Level 2's machine and skip places; and yellow and black hazard tape along the inner edge of the loading-gate apron, whose yellow KEEP CLEAR hatching stays. Floor marking is good practice rather than a legal requirement; keeping escape routes clear is the requirement.
