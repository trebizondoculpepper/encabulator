# Implement Level 0 machine wiring

Implement the wiring and feedback for Level 0, the non-narrative machine sandbox at `?room=level0`. The player experiments with the controls and learns by observing consequences. Deliver a coherent working toy with plausible industrial relationships, rather than a prescribed operating sequence. This is a future implementation prompt; authoring this document does not execute the work.

Follow the repository's `AGENTS.md` and applicable toolkit instructions. Start with `src/rendering/level0-room.ts`, `src/rendering/level0-machine.ts`, `src/game/level0-room-plan.ts`, `src/scene/retro-encabulator-mcc.ts` and `doc/design/world-model.md`. Keep the warehouse layout and independent Level 0 world. Keep narration, scripted mission stages, required random chores and the Level 2 ending out of this level. Retain the chair-to-skip swap on first board removal as specified below. Mechanical and electrical sounds are part of the feedback. Preserve Level 2's scenario behaviour and the development galleries when extending shared components; expose bindings or options where the sandbox needs different wiring.

## Inventory every functional unit and control

Create a wiring inventory in `doc/design/level0-wiring.md`. Identify each functional front-panel unit, its purpose, every existing physical operator control, its world value or action, local feedback, dependencies, fault conditions and recovery policy. Include every selector position, meaningful knob range, pushbutton, operating handle, key switch, TEST, ACK and RESET. Inventory the internal electrical and mechanical components too, including their presence, connection, health, power and outputs.

Every functional front-panel unit needs at least one meaningful physical input on that unit. Add a plausible knob, selector or button where a display-only unit currently has none. Use existing shared parts, legends, catalogue dimensions and the universal input scheme. An existing isolator or purposeful TEST control may already satisfy this requirement. Do not solve every missing input by adding a generic RESET. Passive covers, structural fittings and blank plates need no invented controls; list those exceptions explicitly. Internal self-acting parts need model inputs and dependencies, not artificial operator buttons on each fuse or capacitor.

For Phase Reference, add a physical PHASE TRIM knob or an equivalently plausible local selector. Its setting must visibly affect the displayed signal and a numerical readout where appropriate. The phase-reference LCD is currently a live canvas display in `createLcdMonitor` in `src/toolkit/components/industrial-control/views/cabinet-fittings.ts`: a 320 × 240 texture updated at about 12 Hz, with a vector trace calculated from sine waves and a time-driven phase. Bind the relevant signal parameters to the world model. The knob should change the trace through the reference signal's state; dependent modules can use that same signal.

## Label every button and display

Every button and every display must have a readable in-world label identifying its purpose. This applies to existing and newly added parts, front panels and accessible cabinet interiors. Label operating handles, knobs and selectors too, including their positions or ranges where needed. Displays should identify the quantity or function they show, with units, scale or mode where applicable. Distinguish repeated TEST, ACK and RESET buttons by their local panel context. Labels must describe the actual wired behaviour.

Use the existing physical legend plates and printed-label components. Place labels beside their associated control or on its intended legend area, with sufficient contrast and legible text at normal operating distance. Keep labels within the panel and clear of moving parts, displays and other legends. Printed lettering must not intercept use or obscure the control it identifies. Do not substitute HTML overlays or hover-only text for physical labels. Include label coverage and any naming decisions in the wiring inventory.

## Dimensionality LCD mode

Add one selectable LCD mode called Dimensionality, preferably on the Encabulator Systems display. As the machine powers up, show the progression from one dimension to two, three, four and finally higher-dimensional operation. Use recognisable stages: a line, a square or planar figure, a wireframe cube, a projected rotating tesseract, and increasingly complex higher-dimensional projections. Keep a legible stage/dimension readout so the player can understand the progression. These are live calculated graphics in the existing LCD rendering system, not a video or a prerecorded sequence.

Drive the displayed stage, coherence and relevant signal parameters from the machine's modeled energisation and readiness. Define and document which real modeled startup conditions allow each stage; elapsed time alone must not advance a machine with missing power, required modules or permissives. Allow partial startup to settle at an intermediate stage. Rotation and projection can be presentation, while achieved dimensionality is a world value available to other equipment. Bound geometry complexity and redraw work to keep the LCD readable and inexpensive.

Ensure the startup progression is actually visible after the LCD's own boot messages. Coordinate process startup and display readiness rather than letting all dimensional stages finish unseen during boot or replaying a false history afterward. Repeated reset and restart must show the appropriate loss and rebuilding of readiness. Faults should degrade or interrupt the dimensional state consistently with the faulted dependency. The screen must go dark when its own supply is absent; show any collapse or loss of coherence only while the display has power. Use an in-world mode selector or existing suitable display input through the shared control scheme.

## Make consequences coherent and observable

Every operator control must have a real function. Usually it has an immediate local acknowledgement, followed by the appropriate local physical response: a reading, trace, indication, sound, operating state or diagnostic changes. Keep plausible response times for motor acceleration, pressure, cooling and display booting. TEST and ACK may legitimately operate indicators and alarms rather than the process. An unpowered or interlocked action should have consistent behaviour; do not invent process changes merely to make every press look successful.

Use a small, documented network of plausible dependencies. For example, pump availability can affect drive permissives; cooling can affect a module's availability; a reference source can affect nearby synchronised equipment; Automation power, processor and I/O can affect controlled modules. A major supply or processor failure can have global effects. A local adjustment should not make unrelated instruments jump without a documented cause. The Encabulator's purpose is fictional, but power, isolation, sensing, enable signals, trips and reset behaviour should be intelligible.

Use the world graph as the source of truth. Bind controls, displayed values, sound parameters and fault indications to it. Replace arbitrary wandering readings where they claim to represent a controlled process; limited noise may be presentation of an otherwise meaningful value. Views draw, ease and animate; parts own interaction and sounds; the room assembles and wires them. A knob that only changes a decorative trace while the modeled reference remains unchanged is incomplete. Keep gameplay coupling out of room-specific sound or click handlers.

Preserve useful established logic: incoming isolator before main disconnect; downstream power removal darkens powered displays and brings gauges to zero/default; machine sounds run up and down; displays boot; BOOST drives the panendermic fan's pressure gauge into the red; the Automation cabinet isolator indicates green for ON and red for OFF while its upstream indication supply is available. Preserve the existing attachment rules, shared pickup/drop behaviour and component models rather than replacing them with scripted effects.

## Wire the internal components and their dependencies

Internal components are functional parts of the same model as their panels. A removed, disconnected or failed device must lose the relevant ability to convey power, signals or motion. Model presence/connection, condition and power separately: an isolated healthy board is not failed; a failed board remains failed without power; an absent board cannot be repaired by RESET.

Give different components consequences appropriate to their roles. A missing Automation supply or processor may disable several functions. A missing I/O or analogue card may produce a specific diagnostic, lost readings or unavailable outputs in related modules. Pulling a fuse, losing a terminal connection or failing an internal device should propagate through actual dependencies. Use distinct, traceable fault identities and explanatory LCD messages or codes, annunciator windows and the stack light. Several downstream symptoms can belong to one underlying failure.

Keep cabinet isolation as the requirement for removing installed serviceable parts. Evaluate it against the appropriate local circuit; a loose item remains pickable. Fixed motherboards and board-mounted parts remain firmly attached. If a fixed component fails, service the appropriate replaceable parent assembly rather than making every soldered part detachable. Closing a door or culling its contents must not suspend its model, clear its fault or restore its outputs.

## Spawn the skip when a board is removed

Retain Level 2's skip appearance in the non-narrative sandbox. The first successful removal of an installed circuit board from the machine replaces the chair stack with the skip at the established location. Trigger on actual detachment, whether the board is healthy or faulty; touching a board, a refused pickup while the cabinet is live, opening a door or removing another kind of part must not trigger it. Reuse Level 2's shared skip model, dressing, placement, collisions, positional clatter and bin-tossing components. No narration, camera takeover, mission objective or disposal quota accompanies the appearance.

Spawn the skip once per room instance. It remains present through subsequent board removals, repairs, resets and room changes that preserve the room instance; reseating the first board does not bring the chairs back. Removed boards can be carried, dropped and discarded using the existing shared controls. Throwing a faulty board into the skip does not install a replacement or repair its missing function. Do not spawn additional skips or make a discarded healthy board count as a repaired fault. Include the swap and disposal behaviour in the completion report and verification walkthrough.

## Implement one random component failure at a time

Random failures are included in this implementation. After a few minutes of powered, functioning operation, select an eligible installed, powered, healthy component and put it into a specific faulty state. Use named configurable timing constants; a reasonable initial default is a fresh interval of approximately three to five minutes. Use simulation time, pause with the room, and inject the random source so tests can reproduce the selection and timing. Do not select components that are absent, isolated, already failed or incapable of the chosen fault. Keep existing self-acting fuse/overload behaviour; do not add a separate progressive-wear simulation in this pass.

Allow at most one injected random root-cause failure at a time. Downstream trips and alarms caused by it are consequences of that incident, not additional random failures. Pause new random failures while that incident or other unresolved fault conditions are being investigated and repaired. Resume with a fresh interval after recovery, avoiding an accumulated timer that causes an immediate second failure. Players may still remove several parts; do not block normal interactions just to maintain a single-random-failure rule.

The failure must originate in component state, not in an arbitrary global smoke flag. A missing board normally produces diagnostics and lost function. A suitable energised electrical failure or resulting severe condition may produce sparks and smoke from the actual failed component. Isolation stops new electrical discharge immediately; existing smoke disperses gradually. The component remains failed. If power returns before repair, its failure and dependent diagnostics persist and hazardous effects resume where appropriate. Keep sounds and effects spatially attached to their sources, including a removed failed assembly. Use the existing effects and assets where possible.

## Recovery and reset are defined per unit

Provide a repeatable player-visible isolation, removal, replacement/reseating and restart path for the selected serviceable failures using the existing shared use and pickup/drop scheme. Reuse existing connection and component interfaces; implement only the minimum missing slot/replacement interaction needed for the repair loop. Do not add new keys, gestures, on-screen action buttons or a servicing mission. Give the player an accessible, plausible supply of compatible healthy replacements if replacement requires one; document the placement without redesigning the warehouse. A test-only health mutation alone does not complete a player-facing repair loop. Report any unsupported component/assembly honestly and keep it out of the random pool until it can be repaired.

Record a recovery policy for each unit, based on its actual equipment. Some units have a local RESET and must be reset after their fault's cause is corrected. Others start automatically when power and required dependencies return. ACK acknowledges an alarm; it does not repair hardware. A local reset does not silently repair other modules, replace a missing part or erase a fault whose cause persists.

Automation's RESET is unconditional whenever the cabinet has the power needed to respond: every valid press starts or restarts its reset/boot sequence, including healthy operation and a boot already underway. It must not be guarded by a global machine-stalled flag. A faulty or absent required board is diagnosed again; reset does not manufacture successful operation. Respect ordinary power availability for an electrical button. Propagate the interruption and recovery through Automation's dependencies. Other cabinets follow their documented local reset or automatic-start policy.
