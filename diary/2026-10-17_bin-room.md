| 18:30 UTC | main   | uncommitted | A dev room for throwing circuit boards in a bin |

The owner asked for a development room, Putting Things in the Bin, to model the game's visual gag of disposing of expensive circuit boards in a stupid way: plinths holding the boards we have built round a single large office waste-paper bin; let go of a board facing the bin and within 1.5 m and it should arc into the bin and settle there, otherwise clatter to the floor; boards should keep going in and the bin should fill up. dev13 had meanwhile been taken by another agent's Computer Beeps room, so this is dev14, placed in the chain before the Rhombus mini-game, whose EXIT now leads to it.

The rule, the arc and the filling are pure logic in `src/game/bin-toss.ts` with tests: `aimsAtBin` (within 1.5 m on the floor plan and the bin within 40° of the line of sight, or standing almost over it), `binArc` (a parabola through both ends rising a quarter of a metre over the higher), `arcSeconds` and `binRest` (each landing a layer higher, capped a little above the rim, tilting more steeply as the bin fills, laid roughly along the bin's length). The bin is a true-size copy of a Rubbermaid Slim Jim 87-litre container in `src/scene/office-bin.ts`. The room reuses the shared pickup and drop; `createObjectHandling` gained a `divert` hook, passed through `createFirstPerson`, so a room can take a released object instead of letting it fall. The room flies the board along the arc, turning it from how it was held to lying flat by its own shape (thinnest side up, longest along the bin), tipped up at the end as the bin fills, then calls the board's impact (its own clatter) and settle, adds a plastic thud and locks it in place. Plinths restock three seconds after their board is taken.

What we learned: the drop orientation used for the floor (`flat`) is wrong for the bin, where boards came to rest edge-on; laying each by its measured bounding box is simpler and right for every board. Boards still poke through the bin's thin walls now and then, since nothing collides inside it. Browser checks (load, no console errors, `devBin.throw` filling and overfilling the bin, a dropped card landing on a plinth deck) were done; a full keyboard pick-up-and-toss run was cut short when the shared browser tab was taken over by another agent's work.
