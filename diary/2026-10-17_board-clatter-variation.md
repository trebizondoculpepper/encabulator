# Varied board landing contacts

The collision-driven sound had attenuated secondary bounces so strongly that drops sounded like a single knock. The first landing now starts a bounded 2–5-contact clatter, with impact speed biasing the count and initial volume. Later contacts retain 55–67% of the preceding amplitude, vary their filter/decay, and accelerate over a short irregular sequence. Later simulated bounces do not restart the sequence. A shared Web Audio sequence recipe schedules the contact offsets on the audio clock, avoiding frame-rate-dependent timing. The existing low-level scrape remains.

Tests cover all four contact counts, the relationship to drop speed, strictly decreasing amplitudes and intervals, varied timing/timbre and the half-second settling limit.

Board geometry now supplies an acoustic size when the portable part is assembled, before room placement or daughtercard attachment. Larger boards lower both the impact-noise band and the thump frequency, lengthen the resonance slightly, and produce a lower scrape. Impact speed still controls contact count and loudness independently. Tests compare a 480 mm motherboard with a 160 mm daughterboard under identical drop and random conditions.
