# Pickable dev0 pedestal exhibits

Every one of the eighteen dev0 pedestal exhibits can be picked up, carried at the same distance and dropped through the existing common use action. Wall-mounted controls and displays stay fixed. Existing separately removable expansion cards remain pickable, and the cylindrical fuse cartridge can now be removed independently of its box-shaped holder. Only the gallery enables whole-device portability; MCC installation controls keep their existing behaviour.

The component model owns an immutable, precomputed DropProfile per catalogue kind: shape, size, effective rolling radius, restitution, sliding friction, rolling resistance, skitter amount and impact sound material/frequencies/decay/gain. Flat boards settle quickly; box-shaped housings tumble with diminishing corner contacts and small direction changes; round cartridges roll straight without skitter or bounce chatter. The renderer accounts for the changing bounds as objects turn and maintains floor clearance. New procedural impact and rolling sounds use the same profile as the movement.

Every pickable component must pass profile validation, including sound settings. Tests cover every catalogue profile settling at several frame rates, gallery pickup metadata, invalid profiles, flat/box/round motion differences and restoration of the original parent, position, rotation and scale. The dev0 reset/replace button also resets removed subcomponents and repairs the exhibit; no other room gains this reset behaviour.

Follow-up requests added explicit fixed/detachable model metadata and a temporary pickup lock. Gallery exhibits start locked for operation; the amber LOCK / RELEASE toggle releases each exhibit and its removable subcomponents for pickup. Reset returns and repairs them and re-locks the exhibit. Wall controls remain fixed. The test apparatus is now audited per component: unused supply selectors, input knobs and the terminal block's artificial fault button are omitted, while useful load, voltage, signal and speed controls remain. Labels describe the actual control.

The gallery now has nineteen working exhibits, including an individual 35 × 45 mm Chemi-Con-format capacitor. Its shared component exposes a one-shot failure age, single flash, 3–5 embers and three seconds of smoke, with a permanently blackened, bulging split-vent appearance until replacement. Board-mounted capacitors use the same failure model and view but remain fixed. The pedestal capacitor has a cylindrical drop profile and can roll on its side.

All test controls, titles and catalogue references moved onto the horizontal pale-metal top panel. Titles use a full-width single-line label with black ink; references sit below them. Small device lettering, especially the control relay, is reduced and constrained to its housing width. Ten green PCB textures were baked from the existing procedural generator and applied to both faces of backplanes, power boards and all daughtercard kinds; `npm run bake:pcb` reproduces the assets.

The dev0 room extends 2.5 m farther beyond the last row, retaining the original north wall and wall boards. Five empty pedestals complete the fourth row beside the capacitor. Each carries a folded cream card, readable from either side: EXHIBIT OUT OF SERVICE, with a small note that the curators have temporarily removed it. Their decks participate in the same support and walking-collision layout as the working plinths.

The dev6 motherboard is firmly attached, carries visible chips, and retains five fixed black edge-connector sockets when its expansion cards are removed. The separate dev0 backplane exhibit remains detachable.

Wired dev6's main disconnect to the supply of every bucket, with the initial state off. Screens and indicators blank when unpowered, analogue instruments return to zero, chart paper stops advancing and mechanical hour meters retain their readings. Power restoration gives a 2.4-second blink sequence; a positional procedural drive-train hum rises over two seconds and loses pitch and amplitude over a five-second coast to silence. Existing component failures and local breakers still interrupt their own circuits.

Added per-bucket interior visibility configuration (`MccOptions.interiorVisibility`, keyed by `sectionN.bucketN`), choosing `always` or `when-open`. The windowed PLC-5 cabinet defaults to always rendering its contents; opaque doors keep the existing culling. Moved the 480 V warning and field-terminations warning/nameplate forward 1 mm to prevent surface flicker. Regression tests cover startup, supply propagation, configurable culling and fixed motherboard sockets.

Added original cut-down BSD-style boot diagnostics for dev6's CRTs, PanelView terminal, drive keypad LCD, auxiliary LCD and overhead message display. Each screen owns a 15-second boot clock tied to its local supply; narrow displays scroll two short lines, larger screens show a green console log, and the normal application resumes when boot completes. An interrupted supply blanks the screen and restarts boot. Pure tests verify diagnostic progression, duration, text bounds and restart.
