# Settling objects into skips (dev21)

The owner wants the game to be able to throw a great deal away, and the shared bin tossing fills a skip too soon: each thing lands a layer higher than the last, tipped up more and more, so a skip is full after a few dozen boards. The ask was a development room where an emitter throws something into a skip every second, to see how a skip fills, and a settling rule that makes flat, rectangular things settle into the pile plausibly. Not physics: "it just has to look approximately like things settling into a bin". Offered a physics engine, the owner chose our own algorithm.

The rule (`createSkipPile`, `src/toolkit/components/site-dressing/logic/skip-pile.ts`) keeps a height map of the skip's inside, starting as its floor and sloping ends. A thing is a slab let fall flat at a spot and turn; it rests on the face of the pile's upper hull under its middle (found by trying every three of 15 points under it for the lowest plane above them all with its middle inside), slides downhill off anything steeper than a board would stay on, jams flat if it can slide no further, and is stamped into the height map. The room (`settling-room.ts`) spawns one of dev14's boards on a ring 2 m over the skip, boils it in with `createMaterialise`, flies it to its resting pose and merges it once landed; a skip heaped 0.3 m over its rim is emptied after four seconds.

The first settling let boards lean at up to 50° and they climbed the skip's steep sloping ends, a heap at each end and the middle low; at 35° they slide off and lie flatter, and 120 boards stand under the rim, about 240 before it is heaped. The "top" of the pile first counted the skip's own ends, which reach almost to the rim; it now counts only what has been thrown in. Seen from above the boards lie flat and overlapping, like a real heap. Performance is fine at one a second (a board takes 2 to 8 ms to make; frames under 24 ms); a stress run at ten a second backed up materialise compiles.

Next: the shared bin tossing (dev14, dev15, Level 0 and 2) still uses its layer-by-layer rule; switching it to `createSkipPile` would let every skip in the game take far more.
