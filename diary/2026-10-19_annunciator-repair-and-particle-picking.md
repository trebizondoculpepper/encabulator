# Annunciator repair and particle picking

The annunciator isolator's real Level0 use ray hit an invisible ember sprite before reaching the breaker. Ember sprites carried `noCollision` metadata, but the room's control picker did not consult it. All sprite and point-cloud effects now use shared non-interactive particle classes: sparks, capacitor flashes, embers, smoke, dust, glow halos, extinguisher clouds and sprinkler sprays cannot intercept any raycaster, visible or hidden. Level0 also skips `noCollision` objects when picking controls, and placement helper planes answer only their own placement ray. The global agent rules record the non-interactive particle requirement.

The breaker accepts use on its body as well as its lever, whose pale metal handle now travels visibly between positions. A close development inspection start targets the annunciator isolator. Regression coverage exercises actual cabinet ray hits, supply/link interruption, hidden and visible particles, and placement-plane isolation.

The automation control fuse is now a removable cartridge in fixed clips, with its own persistent failed state and a compatible spare; a normal cabinet reset does not repair a blown fuse. Both Neumann boards are detachable, have matching 90 mm card-edge connectors and sockets, and are stocked on the repair bench. Removing or failing Neumann Diagnostics makes every annunciator window flash amber; either missing Neumann board drops the BBC diagnostics endpoint. Reinsertion accepts only an empty compatible socket and preserves an existing component fault. These changes do not implement component dematerialisation timers or probabilistic damage from live handling.

Card insertion targets now sit at the actual female edge connectors instead of the front of the withdrawn card. Angled-camera tests aim at each of the five automation and two Neumann connectors and require green for the correct carried card and red for an incompatible fuse; the same decision performs insertion.
