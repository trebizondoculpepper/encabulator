# Level 0 machine wiring

Implemented `doc/prompts/level0-wire-machine.md` in milestones, each committed and pushed after the five checks, at the owner's request to save work as it went. The plan is `doc/plans/level0-wiring.md` (the owner asked for plans to live in `doc/plans`), the inventory `doc/design/level0-wiring.md` and the report `doc/reports/level0-wiring-implementation.md`.

The toolkit gained what the wiring needed without changing other rooms: the PLC backplane's slots are wired to whichever card is seated in them, so a card can go back (or a spare of its kind go in); a diverted release is forgotten by the shared object handling, so a board can be installed somewhere new; bound potentiometers; latched key switches and breaker handles (the Kirk key); restartable screen boots; the annunciator's ISA sequence M; LCD signals and room-drawn pictures; and a rocker style for the disconnect handle part, on the existing illuminated rocker view.

The game gained a Level 0 process model on one world graph, the dimensionality stages and their n-cube projections, and a one-at-a-time failure scheduler, all pure and tested; the shared line-up gained optional hooks (`dress`, `interlock`, `breakerLatch`, `toneRate`, `driveTrain`, `stackLight`) and exposes its circuits and doors. Level 0 fits every door itself: an input and labels on every functional unit, PHASE TRIM driving the phase reference's figure and lock, a DIMENSIONALITY mode on ENCABULATOR SYSTEMS, PanelView pages, a working drive keypad with LOCAL and REMOTE, and readings bound to the model in place of wandering ones.

The owner then set the spares bench aside and asked for the machine to be fully wired inside first. Every terminal strip, relay and chassis now has a role (X1 control supply, X2 run circuit, X3 indication, X4 PLC signal, the rest spare; control units' K1 enable, K2 PLC link, K3 indication; FIELD TERMINATIONS' supply contactor and interposing relays; RU1 the PanelView interface, RU2 the remote I/O adapter), and every component reports its own faults through a new CONTROL CCT window. The owner also found identical throw handles on many cabinets and one overprinting MAIN DRIVE MOTOR's lamps: power is now switched by flange handles on the full-size starters, small rockers on the small units and isolators inside elsewhere, and the lamps moved. With no spares, card failures left the random pool; blown control fuses and tripped overloads remain, each repaired by its own part.

Checked in headless Chrome against the dev server: the line-up renders with its new controls and labels; run up through the development hook and stepped ninety simulated seconds, ENCABULATOR SYSTEMS drew the six-cube at 6D with all conditions met and the beacon green; a blown lube pump fuse darkened its lamps, dropped MAIN DRIVE MOTOR on NO LUBE, ran the drive down, collapsed the dimensionality to 2D and raised U31-F1 and A01. Typecheck, lint, formatting, 596 tests and the build passed.

At the owner's request the feed and auxiliary motor panels now each carry both an SMC Plus soft starter display (the shared `createSoftStarter`, ramping on each start) and an emergency stop, in Level 0 and on the shared line-up alike. The feed motor's stop, once struck, keeps the motor stopped until it is pulled out and HAND OFF AUTO is turned to OFF; the auxiliary motor's still needs START. Both panels were laid out again so nothing overlaps, and checked in the browser.

Then the owner's two queued prompts. Level 0 now starts with every machine switch and selector off (`doc/reports/level0-default-controls-off.md`): every operator-worked breaker open, the Kirk key out, the START motors stopped, HAND OFF AUTO, a new OFF on the hydraulic key and on the meter phase selector, the chart stopped and the speed knobs at zero, set once when the machine is made. And the incoming supply's voltages now come from the model (`doc/reports/phase-selector-voltage-feedback.md`): steady and equal at 480 V without load, sagging and gently wandering with each phase pair's current, the L1-L2 pair carrying the control transformer, the selector only choosing which phase the power monitor measures. The shared power monitor stopped dropping a digit from readings of 100 and over.

At the owner's request the Kirk key has a red pilot light beside it under an ILLUMINATED IF LOCKED plate: lit while the key is out and the interlock holds the main disconnect open, fed from the incoming side so that it shows with the disconnect open, and push to test. Pilot light parts now record whether they are lit (`userData.lit`) for tests.

The power monitor on MAIN DISCONNECT now measures downstream of the disconnect: nothing while it is open, then a current that climbs as each unit is switched on (each energised unit's control circuit drawing through the control transformer, as well as the motors), with kW as volts times amps, as the owner asked. The PHASE selector became a three-position control-supply selector that leaves the monitor alone. The owner renamed Level 0's Kirk key POWER INTERLOCK SECURITY (Level 2 keeps its Kirk key, which its narration names), and asked that a locked main disconnect refuse with a noise and a slight shake: every disconnect handle now clunks against its stop and shudders when latched, including those that share a breaker's mechanism, which had refused silently.

The owner turned the annunciator into a module status panel: one window for each of the 23 modules and one for earth and doors, each green when its module is on and OK, flashing green while it starts, amber for a warning, red when failed or switched off, flashing red for a critical failure until ACK, and dark only without the annunciator's own power (`src/game/level0-status.ts`). It scans slowly, as 1980s equipment would: one window every 0.2 s, a recovering window passing through flashing green for a scan before it goes steady. LUBE goes green once the oil pump has built its pressure. Motors on motor controllers show green only once up to speed; the soft-started feed and auxiliary motors draw 24 A each at speed, climbing to it through their 4 s ramp (`softStartedAmps`), shown on their SMC Plus displays and the auxiliary motor's ammeter. Another session's snapshot commit (d2098e1) took in the panel part-way; this commit adds the motor speeds and currents.

Level 0 now draws only the side of the machine in view (`src/scene/machine-side-culling.ts`, committed by another session's 723373c): its back hidden from the front and its doors from behind, both beside the ends, the hidden side taking no clicks. The owner asked for a cheat console, first as a locked box on a bolted post and then, on reflection, as the Hyperplasc Scenario Simulator by Neumann Dynamik: a 42U rack from the dev3 gallery's builder (which now exposes its door and offers smoked perspex), AUTHORISED PERSONNEL ONLY, its key in the lock, with eye-level TESTING SCENARIOS buttons. FULL OFF, FULL ON, SLOW START and RESET work the machine's own switches, actions and repairs (the model gained a `clearFaults` action for faults no component holds); HICCUP uses the line-up's scripted stumble, GLITCH a real fuse or overload fault and KAPUT the rack power card's failure, until the owner's next step models them as real failures.

For the scenario simulator, two years newer than the line-up, the owner asked for a newer family of buttons in the style of high-end studio gear, tried first in dev0. The toolkit now has the studio switch (`createStudioSwitch`, the `studioSwitch` part): a 26 mm satin-graphite bezel round a soft-touch square cap with its top-right corner chamfered, a 1.2 mm press, a square LED window and the legend printed on the cap, momentary (the LED shows the press), latching, or with its LED driven apart, in green, amber, red, blue and white, with a soft high click of its own. dev0's north wall has a STUDIO CONTROLS board (`?view=studio`) showing each, and the seven testing-scenario switches ready for the simulator.

The owner liked the studio switches and asked for them on the simulator: its TESTING SCENARIOS panel is now one row of seven graphite studio switches, each LED showing its press, SLOW START's lit while the start goes on.

The simulator's scenario switches now have coloured caps to stand out against the black rack (FULL OFF and KAPUT red, FULL ON and SLOW START white, RESET blue, HICCUP and GLITCH yellow, each with a contrasting LED). The drive bays' own activity lights flicker green and amber, quickly and at random, as busy drives' do (the rack builder now names them), and the added status LEDs no longer sit over them.
