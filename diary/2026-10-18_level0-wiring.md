# Level 0 machine wiring

Implemented `doc/prompts/level0-wire-machine.md` in milestones, each committed and pushed after the five checks, at the owner's request to save work as it went. The plan is `doc/plans/level0-wiring.md` (the owner asked for plans to live in `doc/plans`), the inventory `doc/design/level0-wiring.md` and the report `doc/reports/level0-wiring-implementation.md`.

The toolkit gained what the wiring needed without changing other rooms: the PLC backplane's slots are wired to whichever card is seated in them, so a card can go back (or a spare of its kind go in); a diverted release is forgotten by the shared object handling, so a board can be installed somewhere new; bound potentiometers; latched key switches and breaker handles (the Kirk key); restartable screen boots; the annunciator's ISA sequence M; LCD signals and room-drawn pictures; and a rocker style for the disconnect handle part, on the existing illuminated rocker view.

The game gained a Level 0 process model on one world graph, the dimensionality stages and their n-cube projections, and a one-at-a-time failure scheduler, all pure and tested; the shared line-up gained optional hooks (`dress`, `interlock`, `breakerLatch`, `toneRate`, `driveTrain`, `stackLight`) and exposes its circuits and doors. Level 0 fits every door itself: an input and labels on every functional unit, PHASE TRIM driving the phase reference's figure and lock, a DIMENSIONALITY mode on ENCABULATOR SYSTEMS, PanelView pages, a working drive keypad with LOCAL and REMOTE, and readings bound to the model in place of wandering ones.

The owner then set the spares bench aside and asked for the machine to be fully wired inside first. Every terminal strip, relay and chassis now has a role (X1 control supply, X2 run circuit, X3 indication, X4 PLC signal, the rest spare; control units' K1 enable, K2 PLC link, K3 indication; FIELD TERMINATIONS' supply contactor and interposing relays; RU1 the PanelView interface, RU2 the remote I/O adapter), and every component reports its own faults through a new CONTROL CCT window. The owner also found identical throw handles on many cabinets and one overprinting MAIN DRIVE MOTOR's lamps: power is now switched by flange handles on the full-size starters, small rockers on the small units and isolators inside elsewhere, and the lamps moved. With no spares, card failures left the random pool; blown control fuses and tripped overloads remain, each repaired by its own part.

Checked in headless Chrome against the dev server: the line-up renders with its new controls and labels; run up through the development hook and stepped ninety simulated seconds, ENCABULATOR SYSTEMS drew the six-cube at 6D with all conditions met and the beacon green; a blown lube pump fuse darkened its lamps, dropped MAIN DRIVE MOTOR on NO LUBE, ran the drive down, collapsed the dimensionality to 2D and raised U31-F1 and A01. Typecheck, lint, formatting, 596 tests and the build passed.

At the owner's request the feed and auxiliary motor panels now each carry both an SMC Plus soft starter display (the shared `createSoftStarter`, ramping on each start) and an emergency stop, in Level 0 and on the shared line-up alike. The feed motor's stop, once struck, keeps the motor stopped until it is pulled out and HAND OFF AUTO is turned to OFF; the auxiliary motor's still needs START. Both panels were laid out again so nothing overlaps, and checked in the browser.

Then the owner's two queued prompts. Level 0 now starts with every machine switch and selector off (`doc/reports/level0-default-controls-off.md`): every operator-worked breaker open, the Kirk key out, the START motors stopped, HAND OFF AUTO, a new OFF on the hydraulic key and on the meter phase selector, the chart stopped and the speed knobs at zero, set once when the machine is made. And the incoming supply's voltages now come from the model (`doc/reports/phase-selector-voltage-feedback.md`): steady and equal at 480 V without load, sagging and gently wandering with each phase pair's current, the L1-L2 pair carrying the control transformer, the selector only choosing which phase the power monitor measures. The shared power monitor stopped dropping a digit from readings of 100 and over.
