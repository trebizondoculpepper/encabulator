# Encabulator: agent guide

This file provides guidance to coding agents (Claude Code, Codex and others) working in this repository. `CLAUDE.md` is a symlink to it.

A browser game about a mysterious machine whose operation is stupid, opaque and profoundly unsafe, after the Rockwell Retro Encabulator film. The game is in English. It was set in an industrial plant in the United States in the late 1990s; it has now moved to England in the present day, to a training room on a trading estate (the training room, below), though the development rooms built for the American plant keep their American parts. It follows the VX3 Simulator (`../vx3_simulator`), from which it took its controls and widgets, its effects, its world graph library and some of its sounds. Stack: Vite+ (`vp`: Vite, Vitest, Oxlint and Oxfmt in one toolchain, configured in `vite.config.ts`), TypeScript, Three.js, `@preact/signals-core`.

For now the game is a series of development rooms that test the concepts the machine will need; the machine itself, and the plant around it, are not built yet.

## Rules

- Work on `main`. Do not create branches or worktrees unless the owner asks.
- Before committing, run `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the same five, and on a push to `main` that passes them it deploys the build to GitHub Pages: https://trebizondoculpepper.github.io/encabulator/. Vite's `base: './'` keeps every asset path relative, so the site works under `/encabulator/`; keep links relative too (`?room=hall`, never `/?room=hall`).
- Markdown is one paragraph per line, never hard-wrapped.
- Record significant sessions in `diary/`.
- Never commit `agent-mailbox.toml` or `agent-inbox.toml`.
- Ask the owner one question at a time.
- Record every sound credit in `CREDITS.md` (author, source URL, licence, edits); technical notes go in `src/audio/assets/README.md`.
- Keep working recordings in `resources/sound/` (`effects/`, `narrations/`, `bgm/`); only prepared game assets belong in `src/audio/assets/`.
- Treat volumes named `NO NAME` as read-only sources; copy files into the workspace before editing them.

## Commands

- `npm run dev`: Vite dev server on `http://127.0.0.1:5174/` (strict port; the VX3 Simulator has 5173).
- `npx vp test run src/game/world/devices.test.ts`: one test file; add `-t "<name>"` for one test. Tests import from `vite-plus/test`, not `vitest`.
- `npm run format`: apply Oxfmt. `npm run check` runs formatting, linting and type checks together (`vp check`).
- Linting is Oxlint, type-aware, with the old ESLint rules carried over (`lint` in `vite.config.ts`); formatting is Oxfmt (`fmt`, single quotes, trailing commas, 80 columns).

## Rooms

`src/main.ts` opens a room from `?room=` and keeps rooms alive while another is shown. The rooms are not one connected world: each is separate, and a portal (a patch of wall in the colour of the room it leads to, named above) sends whoever walks into it, or clicks it from anywhere it can be seen, straight to that room, standing in front of its portal back (`src/game/dev-room-links.ts`, `src/scene/portals.ts`). `src/scene/portal-network.test.ts` checks that every portal in every room can be both walked into and clicked, and leads somewhere with a way back; add a room to `CHAIN` and it is tested too. The rooms form one chain, each linked to its neighbours (`CHAIN`): the training room, whose own door leads to dev0; dev0, dev1, dev2, dev3, dev6 and dev7; and the Rhombus mini-game, whose EXIT door leads back to dev7. The Hall is no longer a hub: it has no portals, nothing links to it, and it is reached only by `?room=hall`.

- **The training room** (`?room=training`, `src/rendering/training-room.ts`): where the game now begins to move, after the owner judged the Hall too expensive to draw. A large, unremarkable, unfurnished meeting room on the ground floor of an English trading-estate office built in the 1990s, today, in the spirit of the original *The Office*, used as the training room for the Retro Encabulator. The company is clean and respectable and leaves nothing in a mess: keep it tidy. It meets present-day UK health and safety standards, and should go on meeting them: emergency lighting with a test key switch (BS 5266), a fire alarm call point at each exit (BS 5839), manifestation and a toughened-glass mark on the glazed fire exit, a vision panel reaching down to 500 mm on the door (Approved Document M), intumescent strips and smoke seals in its frame, the No Smoking sign the law requires, a first-aid notice and an electrical test label on the projector. The building's exterior is not modelled beyond what the windows show. Its plan is `src/game/training-room-plan.ts` (tested: a 14.4 × 9.6 m room on a 600 mm grid, every ceiling fitting and floor box in a tile of its own) and its build is `src/scene/training-room.ts`: magnolia walls, a suspended ceiling of speckled mineral tiles with recessed Category 2 fluorescent fittings, air-conditioning cassettes, smoke detectors, motion sensors and speakers; a hard vinyl floor on a raised access floor with flip-lid floor boxes (BS 1363 sockets and data), all shut; grey aluminium ribbon windows on the south and east walls with top-hung vents on restrictors, vertical blinds and low-surface-temperature radiators; a glazed fire exit with a panic bar in the east glazing under a running-man sign; on the internal walls, dado trunking with sockets, a clean whiteboard under a rolled-up projector screen, a pinboard, a clock, the fire alarm call point, sounder and FIRE ACTION notice, a hearing loop sign, an air-conditioning controller, a "please leave this room as you found it" notice and extinguishers on a stand; stacked chairs, folding tables on edge, a flip chart with a clean pad and a bin. Its door in the north wall (a beech fire door, built in `src/scene/portals.ts`) leads to dev0. Outside, under a grey overcast sky, is the Bracknell Road trading estate, and the view is nothing special: paving, shrub beds and a strip of mown lawn, the estate road with footways and street lights, and across it the other units, plain boxes with rows of windows, with car parks between them. Every parked car is at least 30 m from the windows, so their low polygon counts never show. Everything static is merged (`mergeStatic`), so it draws in about 140 calls and 14,000 triangles. `devTraining.view(room|windows|door|ceiling)`, `devTraining.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` and `devTraining.stats()` help in development; `?view=` takes the same names.

The development rooms:

- **dev0, Controls and Displays** (`/?room=dev0`, the default; `src/rendering/controls-room.ts`). The kinds of controls, displays and working parts the machine will be built from, taken from the motor control centre in the Retro Encabulator film (see `doc/research/retro-encabulator.md`). On the north wall, the CONTROLS board (`createControlsBoard` in `src/rendering/test-boards.ts`) holds one of each 30 mm pilot device, and the DISPLAYS board (`createDisplaysBoard`) holds one of each indicator and display with test controls under it. Along the south wall, a pedestal for each working part of a motor control unit (`src/rendering/device-pedestals.ts`): the unit with its door, operating handle and breaker; a contactor; a thermal overload relay; a control fuse; a drive's power board with its bus capacitors; and an induction motor. Each pedestal has a sloping test panel that puts its part through what it does, failures included. `?view=controls`, `?view=displays` and `?view=pedestals` start in front of each. On the east wall, the CABINET FITTINGS board (`createCabinetBoard`, `?view=cabinet`) tries out the door fittings of the Retro Encabulator (dev6) before they are fitted to it, wired together as on a real unit: a 12-inch CRT monitor with four pictures (trend, mimic, alarm log, vectorscope), a strip-chart recorder, an alarm annunciator with TEST and ACK (`src/game/annunciator.ts`, the ISA flash-acknowledge sequence), a digital power monitor, a soft starter, a pushbutton station, a flange-mounted disconnect handle, louvres, a viewing window, and swatches of the worn pale-blue paint (`src/scene/worn-paint.ts`). The fittings are `src/scene/cabinet-fittings.ts`; new controls and displays are tried on a dev0 board first and then used on the machine, so improving one improves both. In development, `devControls.parts()` lists every clickable part, `devControls.work(name)` works one, `devControls.view(x, y, z, tx, ty, tz)` places the eye and `devControls.log()` reads the log.
- **dev1, Effects** (`/?room=dev1`, `src/rendering/effects-room.ts`): the VX3 Simulator's end-state effects room (colour channels, π drift, warp, sparks, smoke, embers), driven by `src/game/phase-effects.ts` and recorded with `src/game/effect-settings.ts`.
- **dev3, Racks** (`/?room=dev3`, `src/rendering/racks-room.ts`): the industrial design of 19-inch racks, built to catalogue dimensions (`doc/research/rack-dimensions.md`): a bayed suite of 42U server cabinets, an industrial enclosure on its plinth, a 24U cabinet on castors, an open two-post relay rack, a 12U cabinet and an open frame, each branded and filled with units of every kind, and loose units on display plinths with nameplates. It is mostly appearance: a click runs a unit out of its rack on its rails, or back (each unit is a `rackUnit` part). `src/scene/rack-equipment.ts` builds the cabinets (`createRackCabinet`, `createCabinetSuite`, `createRelayRack`) and the units (`createRackUnit`: blank, vented, handles, door, drawer, fans, drives, patch, shelf of half-width units, controls, display). `?view=racks` and `?view=plinths` start in front of each.
- **dev6, Retro Encabulator MCC** (`?room=dev6`, `src/rendering/encabulator-room.ts`): a seven-section Allen-Bradley CENTERLINE-style motor-control lineup, assembled from openable MCC bucket doors and the existing industrial controls, device views and rack units (`src/scene/retro-encabulator-mcc.ts`). It uses 90-inch sections, six-inch wireways and 6.5-inch bucket increments. A wider PLC bay contains period 1771-style I/O and reused rack units; starters expose breakers, contactors, overloads, fuses and terminals. The development room links west to dev3 and east to dev7. Every unit's door has its own face, after the film's line-up and with some licence (`dressDoor`): CRT monitors, a strip-chart recorder, an annunciator, a PanelView-style operator terminal behind a viewing window onto the PLC rack, a drive keypad, a soft starter, power monitors, meters and gauges, LED bar graphs and dot matrices, pushbutton stations, flange handles, louvres, a message display over the control section and a stack light on top; its displays move. Each door has a flat face with a deeper pan behind its middle, and opens only by its two black wing latches on the free edge; everything else on it works as a control (buttons dip, disconnect handles throw, selectors and knobs turn, the mushroom stop latches, the annunciator's TEST and ACK work), so a click meant for a button never opens a door. `devEncabulator.view(...)` and `devEncabulator.door(n)` help in development. The steel is a worn modernist pale blue (`MCC_BLUE`): grime at the edges and feet, hand marks by the handles, chips and scratches, and a base plinth (`MCC_PLINTH`) heavily scuffed by boots and trolleys. `?view=lineup|controls|plc` supplies inspection starts.
- **dev7, Corridor** (`?room=dev7`, `src/rendering/corridor-room.ts`): a zig-zagging basement passage beneath the plant with branches to a locker room, service bay, plant room and lift lobby. The reference photographs are copied into `resources/reference/underground-corridors/`. `src/game/corridor-plan.ts` defines the connected walkable footprint and collision walls; `src/scene/underground-corridor.ts` models painted blockwork, concrete floors, fluorescent fixtures, overhead services, fire doors, signs and connected industrial spaces. The room links west to dev6 and east to the Rhombus mini-game. `?view=entry|dogleg|junction|lockers|lift` supplies inspection starts.
- **The Hall** (`?room=hall`, `src/rendering/hall-room.ts`): the great enclosed hall the game was to take place in; the owner now thinks it too expensive to draw and has set it aside, so no portal leads to it or from it. A 150 × 90 m brutalist American interior, stepped in section (`src/scene/industrial-hall.ts`): a 24 m nave under a coffered waffle slab with skylights, lit by tall vertical clerestory windows, between 10 m side halls under thick slabs on massive columns, with polished concrete underfoot, industrial pendant lamps on chains after Lamp Copper's Astra pendant (`src/scene/pendant-lamps.ts`, instanced), tall windows in the side walls' bays and the end walls, and overcast daylight under a haze. The Hall is drawn by Three's WebGPURenderer (WebGPU where the browser has it, WebGL 2 otherwise) through a node-based render pipeline (`src/rendering/node-pipeline.ts`): one scene pass writes colour, diffuse colour with metalness, normal with roughness, depth and motion; screen-space global illumination (SSGI) adds light bounced between surfaces and ambient occlusion; screen-space reflections (SSR) put the hall in the polished floor and every glossy surface; bloom makes only what is brighter than white glare; temporal anti-aliasing (TRAA) smooths edges and the effects' noise. `?stage=colour|gi|ao|lit|reflections|reflected` shows one stage alone. The building finishes are node materials (`MeshStandardNodeMaterial` with `triplanarTexture`); the Hall's materials must work under WebGPURenderer, so no `onBeforeCompile` shaders there. WebGPU allows only four render targets in a pass (32 bytes a pixel), so add data to the scene pass by packing it into an existing target's spare channel. Round the edges stand ephemera built to standard sizes (`src/scene/warehouse-props.ts`): ISO containers, GMA pallets of cartons, 55-gallon drums, IBC totes, a forklift, janitorial gear, scissor and boom lifts, pallet racking, switchgear, a transformer, a compressor, a gas cage, a workshop bench, bollards, a rolling ladder, a dumpster, and drum and pedestal fans standing switched off. In the north side hall stands the facility administrators' office (`src/scene/plant-office.ts`): steel-stud and drywall partitions with interior window bands and mini-blinds, an open door under a FACILITY ADMINISTRATION sign, a suspended tile ceiling with troffers, and 1990s desks, CRTs, chairs and filing cabinets inside; beside it an amenity block (`src/scene/plant-amenities.ts`) of MEN and WOMEN restrooms (doors a click opens and shuts; inside, tiled, with stalls, urinals, sinks and mirrors), a janitor's closet, a first aid room, a drinking fountain and a time clock. A drivers' toilet, its door also openable, stands by the docks, a glazed security booth overlooks the east personnel door, and an emergency shower and eyewash stands by the chemical store. `doc/research/plant-code-1990s.md` lists what the 1990s codes (OSHA 1910, the regional building codes, NFPA, the ADA) require, and the hall now has it (`src/scene/code-compliance.ts`): extinguishers within 75 ft, exit doors in the side walls with EXIT signs, emergency lights, pull stations and horn-strobes, a battery charging station, a break room with lockers, flammables cabinets, an MSDS station, separate gas cages, a stair to the north balcony and a safety notice board; `devHall.stats()` reports a frame's draw calls, triangles and how much detail is culled. For speed, every static prop and set piece is merged into a few meshes per material in 3 m chunks (`src/scene/merge-static.ts`), the hall's instanced batches (structure, services, markings, lamps) are split into 15 m tiles so off-screen tiles are skipped (`buildTiled` in `src/scene/instancer.ts`), and `src/rendering/detail-culling.ts` hides anything too small to see at its distance and fine detail (chain links, lamp cages, duct seams, hangers, grilles) beyond 35–45 m. New props need nothing extra: they are merged and culled where they are placed; something a click works or that moves must be left out of the merge; the middle of the nave is kept clear for the game. Building services hang from the structure (`src/scene/hall-services.ts`): gas unit heaters, cable trays and exhaust fans. Floor markings (`src/scene/floor-markings.ts`) follow American plant practice: yellow lanes, walkways and cells with arrows and zebra crossings, white storage locations, yellow-and-black hazard stripes, red-and-white keep-clear areas and stencilled words. Both end walls are solid, each with a great door in the nave's end (`src/scene/hall-doors.ts`): the owner chose the yellow high-speed fabric door for the east end and the blue vertical lift fabric door for the west (`devHall.door(n, end)` tries the other styles). Beside each stands a personnel door, open on the white glare of the overcast day, under a lit EXIT sign. The east end also has a truck dock door with bumpers and leveller in each side hall. A physical console desk in the nave (`src/rendering/hall-console.ts`, built on `effects-console.ts`) sets each surface's FINISH, SHADE, ROUGH and METAL (`src/scene/hall-surfaces.ts`, mapped from world position so textures keep their scale), the floor's SHINE (reflections) and BOUNCE (global illumination), the lamps' and sky's LEVEL and KELVIN (`src/game/colour-temperature.ts`), the ambient occlusion, the bloom and the haze; it remembers them in the browser. Control panels are always physical objects in the game, never HTML overlays, unless the owner says otherwise. `devHall.set({...})` changes settings from the console.
- **Rhombus mini-game** (`?room=rhombus`, `src/rendering/plinth-room.ts`): a dark gallery, drawn like the Hall by WebGPURenderer and `src/rendering/node-pipeline.ts`, with three plinths at the corners of a 20 m triangle, about ten seconds' walk apart (`src/game/plinths.ts`, tested). Over each turns a monumental exhibit about a metre tall, in a material of its own (`src/scene/plinth-exhibits.ts`): a rhombic bipyramid of iridescent, dispersive glass round a beating magenta core; a gold double helix with glowing base pairs; and an obsidian sphere veined with molten light, tilted like a planet, its veins drawn from noise in its own frame so its turning shows. The exhibits alone reflect a studio environment; the room stays dark. Walk within 4 m (`REACH`) of a plinth and its spotlight comes up while the others dim; away from all three they stand at a low level, dark enough that arriving plainly lights one (the plinths' stone is lit by its spotlight alone; only the exhibits reflect the studio). Each plinth also has a quiet, other-worldly loop of its own, synthesised and placed where it stands, heard only within 9 m and unrelated to its shape on purpose (`src/audio/plinth-drones.ts`: a struck-glass shimmer at the rhombus, a sliding breath at the helix, a low beating throb at the sphere); a click or key press starts the sound. The tracker records every arrival with the plinth visited just before (`previous`), every one before that (`before`) and how many times this one has been visited (`visit`), and the room dispatches a `plinth-arrival` event with it: the hook for context-sensitive narration selected by `src/game/plinth-narration.ts` and played by `src/audio/plinth-narration.ts` (31 original recorded lines, two intro continuations and 16 additional shape remarks). `devPlinths.view(...)`, `active()`, `previous()` and `arrivals()` help in development; `?view=overview` stands at the south end. The mini-game has one exit: a green steel door with a lit EXIT sign on its east wall, leading to dev7. Glowing yellow floor tape and arrows trace a route from the south approach around the plinths to that door, outside their 4 m activation zones; illuminated EXIT signs on poles mark the route and both turns. The old `?room=dev5` URL remains an alias for `?room=rhombus`; `?room=dev4` remains an alias for the Hall.
- **dev2, Hallucinations** (`/?room=dev2`, `src/rendering/hallucination-room.ts`): peripheral apparitions (`src/game/hallucination.ts`), perceptual ghosting (`src/game/ghosting.ts`, `src/rendering/ghost-overlay.ts`) and geometric patterns (`src/game/patterns.ts`, `src/rendering/pattern-overlay.ts`).

## Architecture

- `src/game/` is pure logic with no DOM or Three.js, with its tests beside it (`*.test.ts`).
- **Components** (`src/game/world/components.ts`) are every part's behaviour, apart from any model of the machine: inputs (Power, Latch), one source of truth for state (a bound world value or its own), guarded operator actions, silent sets from the model, and the events they make (press, on, off, detent, trip, pull-in, blow and the rest), which their part sounds. Operator controls: LED pushbutton, momentary, switch, selector, key-locked selector, operating handle, mushroom stop, push-to-test pilot light. **Self-acting** parts move by what flows through them and sound as they act, whatever moved them: contactor (pull-in, drop-out, welded contacts), thermal overload relay (trip class, RESET once cool, TEST), fuse (I²t, replace), electrolytic capacitor (bulge, vent, smoke, replace), and the hour meter. Use a component for everything a player can work or a failure can strike; failures of board-level parts (a blown capacitor) are components too.
- **Views** (`src/scene/`) only draw. `industrial-controls.ts`: 30 mm push buttons, mushroom head, lever and key selectors, potentiometer, pilot lamp, legend plates and nameplates. `industrial-displays.ts`: stack light, hour meter, message display, operator terminal, drive keypad. `industrial-devices.ts`: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse holder, capacitor board and motor. The 1980s parts from the VX3 Simulator (`eighties-controls.ts`, `panel-switchgear.ts`, `panel-instruments.ts`) are kept and reused.
- **Parts** (`src/rendering/parts.ts`) are the controls and devices as whole things, like React components: each is made from its props alone (colours, legend, what it does, and its bindings to the world: `bound`, `power`, `latch`) and brings its look (the views), its behaviour (the component), what a click on it does and its own sounds, defined beside it as recordings or synthesised clicks and held loops (`src/audio/part-loops.ts`). Bound, a part shows and writes a world value; unbound, it keeps its own, so an LED pushbutton wired to nothing still toggles. Parts play through one shared, positional audio output (`src/audio/part-audio.ts`), which starts at the first click or key press; a room only calls `partAudio.listen(camera, metre)` each frame. A room adds its parts to a `PartSet` for their clicks and updates and never wires a sound or a click by hand; there is no table of sounds anywhere. There are parts for push buttons, LED pushbuttons, pilot lights, lever and key selectors, potentiometers, the emergency stop, disconnect handles, key switches, pushbutton stations, the drive keypad and operator terminal, door latches, rack-mount units (a click runs one out of its rack; a drawer rattles), and the devices: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse, capacitor board and motor. dev0, dev3 and dev6 are built from them.
- **The world graph** (`src/game/world/graph.ts`) is a directed graph of cached functions on signals: inputs, fixed parameters, states, derived values and outputs, with declared actions and processes, and `analyse()` to list dead controls and unseen values. The machine will be built on it. How state divides between the world, derived physics and presentation, and how parts bind to it, is in `doc/design/world-model.md`.
- Every knob and rotary selector turns the same way: a click on its left half turns it anticlockwise (down), its right half clockwise (up), one detent per click (`knobDirection` in `src/scene/rotary-knob.ts`).
- `src/rendering/first-person.ts` walks, looks and zooms. The game aims for realism: the VX3's PS1 look is retired. `src/rendering/ps1-material.ts` keeps its old name but now only makes the realistic standard material for the WebGL rooms (dev0 to dev3), which are still to move to WebGPURenderer like the Hall.
- Mains in the development rooms is American: 60 Hz, 480 V three-phase power, 120 V control. In the training room it is British: 230 V, 50 Hz, BS 1363 sockets.
- Everything is in English: labels, legends, log lines and notes. Some text carried over from the VX3 Simulator may still be German; translate it when found.
