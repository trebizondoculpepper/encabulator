# Encabulator: agent guide

This file provides guidance to coding agents (Claude Code, Codex and others) working in this repository. `CLAUDE.md` is a symlink to it.

A browser game about a mysterious machine whose operation is stupid, opaque and profoundly unsafe, after the Rockwell Retro Encabulator film. The game is in English. It was set in an industrial plant in the United States in the late 1990s; it has now moved to England in the present day, to a training room on a trading estate (the training room, below), though the development rooms built for the American plant keep their American parts. It follows the VX3 Simulator (`../vx3_simulator`), from which it took its controls and widgets, its effects, its world graph library and some of its sounds. Stack: Vite+ (`vp`: Vite, Vitest, Oxlint and Oxfmt in one toolchain, configured in `vite.config.ts`), TypeScript, Three.js, `@preact/signals-core`.

For now the game is a series of development rooms that test the concepts the machine will need; the machine itself, and the plant around it, are not built yet.

## Rules

- Work on `main`. Do not create branches or worktrees unless the owner asks.
- Before committing, run `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the same five, and on a push to `main` that passes them it deploys the build to GitHub Pages: https://trebizondoculpepper.github.io/encabulator/. Vite's `base: './'` keeps every asset path relative, so the site works under `/encabulator/`; keep links relative too (`?room=hall`, never `/?room=hall`).
- Markdown is one paragraph per line, never hard-wrapped.
- Record significant sessions in `diary/`.
- Never commit `agent-mailbox.toml` or `agent-inbox.toml`.
- Ask the owner one question at a time.
- Record every sound credit in `CREDITS.md` (author, source URL, licence, edits); technical notes go in `src/audio/assets/README.md`.
- Keep working recordings in `resources/sound/` (`effects/`, `narrations/`, `bgm/`); only prepared game assets belong in `src/audio/assets/`.
- Treat volumes named `NO NAME` as read-only sources; copy files into the workspace before editing them.
- All controls must be consistent: every room, development rooms included, is walked and worked by the one control scheme in `src/game/controls.ts` (keyboard and mouse, trackpad, touch and gamepad), implemented once in `src/rendering/first-person.ts`. A room never binds a key, button, gesture or stick itself and never writes its own controls help; it only says what a "use" does. To change a control, change the scheme and `first-person.ts`, for every room at once.
- Custom controls are prohibited. No room, feature or tool may add its own input handling: no extra key bindings or shortcuts, mouse or wheel gestures, touch gestures or on-screen buttons, gamepad bindings, or alternative ways to walk, look, zoom or use. Anything new a player must be able to do is added to the one scheme, for every input and every room, or not at all. (This is about input. The machine's in-world control panels, built from parts, are what "use" works, and are not custom controls.) Development hooks on `window` (`devTraining`, `devControls` and the like) are for development only and bind no input.

## Commands

- `npm run dev`: Vite dev server on `http://127.0.0.1:5174/` (strict port; the VX3 Simulator has 5173).
- `npx vp test run src/game/world/devices.test.ts`: one test file; add `-t "<name>"` for one test. Tests import from `vite-plus/test`, not `vitest`.
- `npm run format`: apply Oxfmt. `npm run check` runs formatting, linting and type checks together (`vp check`).
- Linting is Oxlint, type-aware, with the old ESLint rules carried over (`lint` in `vite.config.ts`); formatting is Oxfmt (`fmt`, single quotes, trailing commas, 80 columns).

## Rooms

`src/main.ts` opens a room from `?room=` and keeps rooms alive while another is shown. The rooms are not one connected world: each is separate, and a portal (a patch of wall in the colour of the room it leads to, named above) sends whoever walks into it, or clicks it from anywhere it can be seen, straight to that room, standing in front of its portal back (`src/game/dev-room-links.ts`, `src/scene/portals.ts`). `src/scene/portal-network.test.ts` checks that every portal in every room can be both walked into and clicked, and leads somewhere with a way back; add a room to `CHAIN` and it is tested too. The rooms form one chain, each linked to its neighbours (`CHAIN`): the training room, whose own door leads to dev0; dev0, dev1, dev2, dev3, dev6, dev7, dev8 and dev9; and the Rhombus mini-game, whose EXIT door leads back to dev9. The Hall is no longer a hub: it has no portals, nothing links to it, and it is reached only by `?room=hall`.

- **The training room** (`?room=training`, `src/rendering/training-room.ts`): where the game now begins to move, after the owner judged the Hall too expensive to draw. A large, unremarkable, unfurnished meeting room on the ground floor of an English trading-estate office built in the 1990s, today, in the spirit of the original *The Office*, used as the training room for the Retro Encabulator. The company is clean and respectable and leaves nothing in a mess: keep it tidy. It meets present-day UK health and safety standards, and should go on meeting them: emergency lighting with a test key switch (BS 5266), a fire alarm call point at each exit (BS 5839), manifestation and a toughened-glass mark on the glazed fire exit, a vision panel reaching down to 500 mm on the door (Approved Document M), intumescent strips and smoke seals in its frame, the No Smoking sign the law requires, a first-aid notice and an electrical test label on the projector. The building's exterior is not modelled beyond what the windows show. Its plan is `src/game/training-room-plan.ts` (tested: a 14.4 × 9.6 m room on a 600 mm grid, every ceiling fitting and floor box in a tile of its own) and its build is `src/scene/training-room.ts`: magnolia walls, a suspended ceiling of speckled mineral tiles with recessed Category 2 fluorescent fittings, air-conditioning cassettes, smoke detectors, motion sensors and speakers; a hard vinyl floor on a raised access floor with flip-lid floor boxes (BS 1363 sockets and data), all shut; grey aluminium ribbon windows on the south and east walls with top-hung vents on restrictors, vertical blinds and low-surface-temperature radiators; a glazed fire exit with a panic bar in the east glazing under a running-man sign; on the internal walls, dado trunking with sockets, a clean whiteboard under a rolled-up projector screen, a pinboard, a clock, the fire alarm call point, sounder and FIRE ACTION notice, a hearing loop sign, an air-conditioning controller, a "please leave this room as you found it" notice and extinguishers on a stand; stacked chairs, folding tables on edge, a flip chart with a clean pad and a bin. Its door in the north wall (a beech fire door, built in `src/scene/portals.ts`) leads to dev0. Outside, under a grey overcast sky, is the Bracknell Road trading estate, and the view is nothing special: paving, shrub beds and a strip of mown lawn, the estate road with footways and street lights, and across it the other units, plain boxes with rows of windows, with car parks between them. Every parked car is at least 30 m from the windows, so their low polygon counts never show. Everything static is merged (`mergeStatic`), so it draws in about 140 calls and 14,000 triangles. `devTraining.view(room|windows|door|ceiling)`, `devTraining.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` and `devTraining.stats()` help in development; `?view=` takes the same names.

The reference photographs are copied into `resources/reference/underground-corridors/`. `src/game/corridor-plan.ts` defines the connected walkable footprint and collision walls; `src/scene/underground-corridor.ts` models painted blockwork, concrete floors, fluorescent fixtures, overhead services, fire doors, signs and connected industrial spaces. - **dev8, Building Fittings** (`?room=dev8`, `src/rendering/fittings-room.ts`): the fittings of a present-day English office building, each a working part (`src/rendering/building-parts.ts`, drawn by `src/scene/building-fittings.ts`), all bound to the room's own world model of its building services (`src/game/building-services.ts`, tested), so they do what they would in a real building; new building fittings are tried here first and then used in the training room, as dev0 does for the machine. Each development room may have a world model of its own like this one. North wall, LIGHTING: two-way switching (a switch here and one at the far end, on the south wall), a dimmer, the emergency lighting test key switch, a maintained exit sign, an occupancy sensor that turns the lights off after 90 s without movement, and non-maintained emergency lights that light when the lighting circuit fails or is tested; ACCESSIBLE WC: a pull-cord light, an extractor with run-on, and the emergency pull cord, reset button and over-door light. In the middle of the room, the dev room's joke: a steel waste-paper bin under the smoke detector, and beside it a button post copied from catalogue parts (a galvanised steel post on a bolted base plate carrying a Schneider XALD01-style single-hole control station, 68 × 68 × 53 mm, light grey with a dark grey lid), facing away from the bin so whoever presses its LIGHT THE BIN button, a red illuminated push button lit while the bin burns, is looking at the bin, under a yellow DO NOT PRESS THIS BUTTON sign clamped to the post; it sets off the whole sequence of a two-stage fire alarm with a voice alarm, as in a UK office (BS 5839-1 and -8). Lit, the bin smoulders for a few seconds, smoking a little, then flames catch and grow (particle flames, yellow at the root, orange and red as they rise, drawn additively), and their smoke, rising in a plume and spreading under the ceiling, sets off the detector; in the alert stage the fire doors' holders release and the voice alarm (a ceiling speaker) sounds the alert tone and asks everyone to stay where they are while it is investigated, for 90 seconds, the panel's output delays lamp flashing. The investigation finds, at random, a fire or a false alarm; a false alarm is announced while the bin still burns, so its smoke soon sets the detector off again and the second activation goes straight to evacuation. In evacuation the voice alarm plays the evacuation signal (the VX3 Simulator's synthesised alarm, `src/audio/fire-alarm.ts`) and the evacuation message, the access door unlocks, the brigade is called (the panel's remote output lamp), and the fire grows until the hot gas at the sprinkler head over the bin reaches 68 °C, about 18 seconds in: its glass bulb bursts, it sprays, the sprinkler alarm bell rings, and the water puts the bin out within a quarter of a minute. A call point, or water flowing, goes straight to evacuation. The messages are recordings of a calm British male voice (`src/audio/assets/voice-alarm/`, credited in `CREDITS.md`). The panel is a two-zone conventional panel modelled on a common British one (C-TEC's CFP: an off-white case, a charcoal facia with printed instructions, numbered keys, colour-coded white, red and green buttons, a red key standing out of a keyhole in the facia, and fire, zone and general lamps), under a maker's name of our own: its red button silences or resounds, its green resets (only once nothing is still in alarm, then the all-clear is announced), both only with the key turned; its white silences its own buzzer and key 4 tests its lamps. At the valve set the main valve stops the water and RESET replaces burst heads. On the east wall, FIRE: the panel, a resettable call point, sounder-beacons, water, foam, CO₂ and powder extinguishers (a click pulls the pin, the next discharges it), and a fire door on a magnetic holder. South wall, POWER and CLIMATE: a distribution board (main switch and lighting, sockets and HVAC breakers), a twin socket with testers plugged in, a fused spur, a floor box, the air-conditioning controller and its ceiling cassette, a dial thermostat, a radiator with its thermostatic valve and a window with a vent and blinds; the room's temperature follows from them all. West wall, ACCESS: a maglocked door released by a card reader, push-to-exit or the green emergency door release (reset by its key), which also unlocks in a fire, and an intercom. The room's own lights are the model's. `devFittings.model` is the model, `devFittings.view(bin|overview|lighting|fire|power|access)` (the room starts at `bin`, looking past the pedestal at the bin) and `devFittings.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` place the eye; `?view=` takes the same names.
- **dev9, The Wandering Button** (`?room=dev9`, `src/rendering/wandering-room.ts`): a red illuminated push button (the XB4-style part) in an XALD-style control station on a mobile floor stand (a weighted steel base on four swivel castors that turn to the way it rolls and rumble on the floor, and a galvanised post with a gland-fitted cable loop entering a rubber grommet, and mounting bolts; `createMobileStand` in `src/scene/building-fittings.ts`), and on the north wall a 300 × 300 × 200 mm steel mode panel after PanelSeT CRN, with one four-position cam MODE selector (NORMAL, EAGER, SKITTISH, TERRIFIED; after Harmony K1D004NLH), four labelled XB4 active-mode pilot lights beneath it and a separate amber PRESSED lamp, all devices at true size. Its behaviour is tested pure logic in `src/game/mobile-pedestal.ts`, with the room's small world model: NORMAL, it stays in the middle of the room (going back there at a creep); EAGER, it creeps at a quarter of walking speed to the spot on the floor that puts its red button nearest the middle of your view, no nearer than pressing distance and within the walls; when that sight-line target is unreachable, it approaches until its lens is 0.5 m away, continuously turning to face the player (or uses the nearest clear spot if a wall blocks that space). It waits a random 1–4 seconds of still camera (ignoring tiny jitter), then turns its red face square-on to the viewing axis and travels at the same time, both easing up and down with maximum speeds of 1.5 rad/s and 0.5 m/s; SKITTISH, when you come within 2.5 m it rolls away, always a little faster than you are moving (walking or running), choosing the way furthest from you and clear of the walls, and turns its button away, so it can only be caught in a corner; TERRIFIED, it first heads to the furthest corner and then, when approached there, escapes to another randomly chosen corner, avoiding the player on the way. During the last two metres of each corner journey it turns to face the room centre and keeps that orientation while waiting. After three such escapes (the initial trip does not count) it stops to be pressed; pressing it produces a brief spray of sparks and returns its mode, selector and active lamp to NORMAL, dormant where it stands until another mode is selected. The red button lamp is driven independently of presses: off in NORMAL, a double-pulse heartbeat at 60 bpm in EAGER, 90 bpm in SKITTISH and 120 bpm in TERRIFIED. The separate PRESSED wall lamp still records presses for three seconds. Its castors play a credited, looped shopping-cart recording while it moves or turns. You cannot walk through it, nor it through you. `devWandering.model`, `devWandering.stand()`, `devWandering.view(room|knob|button)`, `devWandering.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)`, `devWandering.probe(x, y)` (what a click at that screen point hits, and whether it works it) and `devWandering.use(x, y)` help in development.
- **The Hall** (`?room=hall`, `src/rendering/hall-room.ts`): the great enclosed hall the game was to take place in; the owner now thinks it too expensive to draw and has set it aside, so no portal leads to it or from it. A 150 × 90 m brutalist American interior, stepped in section (`src/scene/industrial-hall.ts`): a 24 m nave under a coffered waffle slab with skylights, lit by tall vertical clerestory windows, between 10 m side halls under thick slabs on massive columns, with polished concrete underfoot, industrial pendant lamps on chains after Lamp Copper's Astra pendant (`src/scene/pendant-lamps.ts`, instanced), tall windows in the side walls' bays and the end walls, and overcast daylight under a haze. The Hall is drawn by Three's WebGPURenderer (WebGPU where the browser has it, WebGL 2 otherwise) through a node-based render pipeline (`src/rendering/node-pipeline.ts`): one scene pass writes colour, diffuse colour with metalness, normal with roughness, depth and motion; screen-space global illumination (SSGI) adds light bounced between surfaces and ambient occlusion; screen-space reflections (SSR) put the hall in the polished floor and every glossy surface; bloom makes only what is brighter than white glare; temporal anti-aliasing (TRAA) smooths edges and the effects' noise. `?stage=colour|gi|ao|lit|reflections|reflected` shows one stage alone. The building finishes are node materials (`MeshStandardNodeMaterial` with `triplanarTexture`); the Hall's materials must work under WebGPURenderer, so no `onBeforeCompile` shaders there. WebGPU allows only four render targets in a pass (32 bytes a pixel), so add data to the scene pass by packing it into an existing target's spare channel. Round the edges stand ephemera built to standard sizes (`src/scene/warehouse-props.ts`): ISO containers, GMA pallets of cartons, 55-gallon drums, IBC totes, a forklift, janitorial gear, scissor and boom lifts, pallet racking, switchgear, a transformer, a compressor, a gas cage, a workshop bench, bollards, a rolling ladder, a dumpster, and drum and pedestal fans standing switched off. In the north side hall stands the facility administrators' office (`src/scene/plant-office.ts`): steel-stud and drywall partitions with interior window bands and mini-blinds, an open door under a FACILITY ADMINISTRATION sign, a suspended tile ceiling with troffers, and 1990s desks, CRTs, chairs and filing cabinets inside; beside it an amenity block (`src/scene/plant-amenities.ts`) of MEN and WOMEN restrooms (doors a click opens and shuts; inside, tiled, with stalls, urinals, sinks and mirrors), a janitor's closet, a first aid room, a drinking fountain and a time clock. A drivers' toilet, its door also openable, stands by the docks, a glazed security booth overlooks the east personnel door, and an emergency shower and eyewash stands by the chemical store. `doc/research/plant-code-1990s.md` lists what the 1990s codes (OSHA 1910, the regional building codes, NFPA, the ADA) require, and the hall now has it (`src/scene/code-compliance.ts`): extinguishers within 75 ft, exit doors in the side walls with EXIT signs, emergency lights, pull stations and horn-strobes, a battery charging station, a break room with lockers, flammables cabinets, an MSDS station, separate gas cages, a stair to the north balcony and a safety notice board; `devHall.stats()` reports a frame's draw calls, triangles and how much detail is culled. For speed, every static prop and set piece is merged into a few meshes per material in 3 m chunks (`src/scene/merge-static.ts`), the hall's instanced batches (structure, services, markings, lamps) are split into 15 m tiles so off-screen tiles are skipped (`buildTiled` in `src/scene/instancer.ts`), and `src/rendering/detail-culling.ts` hides anything too small to see at its distance and fine detail (chain links, lamp cages, duct seams, hangers, grilles) beyond 35–45 m. Both end walls are solid, each with a great door in the nave's end (`src/scene/hall-doors.ts`): the owner chose the yellow high-speed fabric door for the east end and the blue vertical lift fabric door for the west (`devHall.door(n, end)` tries the other styles). Beside each stands a personnel door, open on the white glare of the overcast day, under a lit EXIT sign. The east end also has a truck dock door with bumpers and leveller in each side hall. A physical console desk in the nave (`src/rendering/hall-console.ts`, built on `effects-console.ts`) sets each surface's FINISH, SHADE, ROUGH and METAL (`src/scene/hall-surfaces.ts`, mapped from world position so textures keep their scale), the floor's SHINE (reflections) and BOUNCE (global illumination), the lamps' and sky's LEVEL and KELVIN (`src/game/colour-temperature.ts`), the ambient occlusion, the bloom and the haze; it remembers them in the browser. - **Rhombus mini-game** (`?room=rhombus`, `src/rendering/plinth-room.ts`): a dark gallery, drawn like the Hall by WebGPURenderer and `src/rendering/node-pipeline.ts`, with three plinths at the corners of a 20 m triangle, about ten seconds' walk apart (`src/game/plinths.ts`, tested). The exhibits alone reflect a studio environment; the room stays dark. ## Architecture

- `src/game/` is pure logic with no DOM or Three.js, with its tests beside it (`*.test.ts`).
- **Components** (`src/game/world/components.ts`) are every part's behaviour, apart from any model of the machine: inputs (Power, Latch), one source of truth for state (a bound world value or its own), guarded operator actions, silent sets from the model, and the events they make (press, on, off, detent, trip, pull-in, blow and the rest), which their part sounds. Operator controls: LED pushbutton, momentary, switch, selector, key-locked selector, operating handle, mushroom stop, push-to-test pilot light. **Self-acting** parts move by what flows through them and sound as they act, whatever moved them: contactor (pull-in, drop-out, welded contacts), thermal overload relay (trip class, RESET once cool, TEST), fuse (I²t, replace), electrolytic capacitor (bulge, vent, smoke, replace), and the hour meter. Use a component for everything a player can work or a failure can strike; failures of board-level parts (a blown capacitor) are components too.
- **Views** (`src/scene/`) only draw. `industrial-controls.ts`: 30 mm push buttons, mushroom head, lever and key selectors, potentiometer, pilot lamp, legend plates and nameplates. `industrial-displays.ts`: stack light, hour meter, message display, operator terminal, drive keypad. `industrial-devices.ts`: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse holder, capacitor board and motor. The 1980s parts from the VX3 Simulator (`eighties-controls.ts`, `panel-switchgear.ts`, `panel-instruments.ts`) are kept and reused.
- **Parts** (`src/rendering/parts.ts`) are the controls and devices as whole things, like React components: each is made from its props alone (colours, legend, what it does, and its bindings to the world: `bound`, `power`, `latch`) and brings its look (the views), its behaviour (the component), what a click on it does and its own sounds, defined beside it as recordings or synthesised clicks and held loops (`src/audio/part-loops.ts`). Bound, a part shows and writes a world value; unbound, it keeps its own, so an LED pushbutton wired to nothing still toggles. Parts play through one shared, positional audio output (`src/audio/part-audio.ts`), which starts at the first click or key press; a room only calls `partAudio.listen(camera, metre)` each frame. A room adds its parts to a `PartSet` for their clicks and updates and never wires a sound or a click by hand; there is no table of sounds anywhere. There are parts for push buttons, LED pushbuttons, pilot lights, lever and key selectors, potentiometers, the emergency stop, disconnect handles, key switches, pushbutton stations, the drive keypad and operator terminal, door latches, rack-mount units (a click runs one out of its rack; a drawer rattles), and the devices: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse, capacitor board and motor. dev0, dev3 and dev6 are built from them; the building fittings in `src/rendering/building-parts.ts` (dev8) are built the same way, on `beginPart`.
- **The world graph** (`src/game/world/graph.ts`) is a directed graph of cached functions on signals: inputs, fixed parameters, states, derived values and outputs, with declared actions and processes, and `analyse()` to list dead controls and unseen values. The machine will be built on it. How state divides between the world, derived physics and presentation, and how parts bind to it, is in `doc/design/world-model.md`.
- Printed lettering (`createMicroLabel`) never takes a click: its sheet is larger than its letters, and it must not block the control it labels. A tap works what the finger first touched, and a touch must move 12 px (a mouse 5) before it counts as a drag.
- Every knob and rotary selector turns the same way: a click on its left half turns it anticlockwise (down), its right half clockwise (up), one detent per click (`knobDirection` in `src/scene/rotary-knob.ts`).
- **Controls** (`src/game/controls.ts`, `src/rendering/first-person.ts`): one scheme for every room. Keyboard and mouse: WASD or arrows walk, Shift runs, a click captures the mouse to look, a click or E uses, a click on a knob's left or right half turns it down or up, the wheel (or a trackpad pinch) zooms, C and V crouch, Alt+wheel sets the eye height, Esc frees the cursor. Touch: a left thumbstick walks (pushed to the rim, runs), a right thumbstick or a drag looks, a tap uses (a knob's left or right half turns it), a two-finger pinch zooms, and a CROUCH button sits above the left stick. Gamepad (standard mapping): the left stick walks (click it to run), the right stick looks, A uses, LB and RB turn a knob down and up (`turningKnobs` in `src/scene/rotary-knob.ts`), the triggers zoom, B crouches. The controls follow the last input used, and a help line at the foot of the screen (`controlsHelp`) shows that input's controls.
- Model fittings on real catalogue parts, at true size: find the product in a manufacturer's catalogue or datasheet (Schneider Electric's are good) and copy its dimensions and look, under a maker's name of our own. Always use the correct-sized component, and never scale a part to fit: a small control station (Schneider XALD) takes 22 mm devices, so it gets the XB4 series (`series: 'xb4'` on the selector and pilot light parts, `createXB4Selector` and `createXB4PilotLight` in `src/scene/industrial-controls.ts`), not the 30 mm oiltight devices shrunk. The illuminated push button (`createPushButton`, head `illuminated`) copies Schneider's Harmony XB4 (XB4BW34M5): a chromed bezel about 30 mm across, 13.5 mm deep, round a flush, faintly domed lens lit from within; the fire alarm panel copies C-TEC's CFP.
- `src/rendering/first-person.ts` walks, looks and zooms. The game aims for realism: the VX3's PS1 look is retired. `src/rendering/ps1-material.ts` keeps its old name but now only makes the realistic standard material for the WebGL rooms (dev0 to dev3), which are still to move to WebGPURenderer like the Hall.
- Mains in the development rooms is American: 60 Hz, 480 V three-phase power, 120 V control. In the training room it is British: 230 V, 50 Hz, BS 1363 sockets.
- Everything is in English: labels, legends, log lines and notes. Some text carried over from the VX3 Simulator may still be German; translate it when found.
