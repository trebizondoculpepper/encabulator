# Encabulator: agent guide

This file provides guidance to coding agents (Claude Code, Codex and others) working in this repository. `CLAUDE.md` is a symlink to it.

A browser game about a mysterious machine whose operation is stupid, opaque and profoundly unsafe, after the Rockwell Retro Encabulator film. The game is in English. It was set in an industrial plant in the United States in the late 1990s; it has now moved to England in the present day, to a training room on a trading estate (the training room, below), though the development rooms built for the American plant keep their American parts. It follows the VX3 Simulator (`../vx3_simulator`), from which it took its controls and widgets, its effects, its world graph library and some of its sounds. Stack: Vite+ (`vp`: Vite, Vitest, Oxlint and Oxfmt in one toolchain, configured in `vite.config.ts`), TypeScript, Three.js, `@preact/signals-core`.

For now the game is a series of development rooms that test the concepts the machine will need; the machine itself, and the plant around it, are not built yet.

## Rules

- Work on `main`. Do not create branches or worktrees unless the owner asks.
- Before committing, run `npm run typecheck`, `npm run lint`, `npm run format:check`, `npm test` and `npm run build`. CI (`.github/workflows/ci.yml`) runs the same five, and on a push to `main` that passes them it deploys the build to GitHub Pages: https://trebizondoculpepper.github.io/encabulator/. Vite's `base: './'` keeps every asset path relative, so the site works under `/encabulator/`; keep links relative too (`?room=hall`, never `/?room=hall`).
- Markdown is one paragraph per line, never hard-wrapped.
- Record significant sessions in `diary/`.
- Never commit `agent-mailbox.toml` or `agent-inbox.toml`.
- Ask the owner one question at a time.
- Record every sound credit in `CREDITS.md` (author, source URL, licence, edits); technical notes go in `src/audio/assets/README.md`.
- Keep working recordings in `resources/sound/` (`effects/`, `narrations/`, `bgm/`); only prepared game assets belong in `src/audio/assets/`.
- Treat volumes named `NO NAME` as read-only sources; copy files into the workspace before editing them.
- All controls must be consistent: every room, development rooms included, is walked and worked by the one control scheme in `src/game/controls.ts` (keyboard and mouse, trackpad, touch and gamepad), implemented once in `src/rendering/first-person.ts`. A room never binds a key, button, gesture or stick itself and never writes its own controls help; it only says what a "use" does. To change a control, change the scheme and `first-person.ts`, for every room at once.
- Custom controls are prohibited. (One exception, by the owner's leave: dev15's on-screen SPAWN SKIP test button.) No room, feature or tool may add its own input handling: no extra key bindings or shortcuts, mouse or wheel gestures, touch gestures or on-screen buttons, gamepad bindings, or alternative ways to walk, look, zoom or use. Anything new a player must be able to do is added to the one scheme, for every input and every room, or not at all. (This is about input. The machine's in-world control panels, built from parts, are what "use" works, and are not custom controls.) Development hooks on `window` (`devTraining`, `devControls` and the like) are for development only and bind no input.

## Commands

- `npm run dev`: Vite dev server on `http://127.0.0.1:5174/` (strict port; the VX3 Simulator has 5173).
- `npx vp test run src/game/world/devices.test.ts`: one test file; add `-t "<name>"` for one test. Tests import from `vite-plus/test`, not `vitest`.
- `npm run format`: apply Oxfmt. `npm run check` runs formatting, linting and type checks together (`vp check`).
- Linting is Oxlint, type-aware, with the old ESLint rules carried over (`lint` in `vite.config.ts`); formatting is Oxfmt (`fmt`, single quotes, trailing commas, 80 columns).

## Rooms

`src/main.ts` opens a room from `?room=` and keeps rooms alive while another is shown. The rooms are not one connected world: each is separate, and a portal (a patch of wall in the colour of the room it leads to, named above) sends whoever walks into it, or clicks it from anywhere it can be seen, straight to that room, standing in front of its portal back (`src/game/dev-room-links.ts`, `src/scene/portals.ts`). `src/scene/portal-network.test.ts` checks that every portal in every room can be both walked into and clicked, and leads somewhere with a way back; add a room to `CHAIN` and it is tested too. The rooms form one chain, each linked to its neighbours (`CHAIN`): the training room, whose own door leads to dev0; dev0, dev1, dev2, dev3, dev6, dev7, dev8, dev9, dev10, dev11, dev12, dev13, dev14, dev15, dev16, dev17 and dev18; and the Rhombus mini-game, whose EXIT door leads back to dev18. The Hall is no longer a hub: it has no portals, nothing links to it, and it is reached only by `?room=hall`.

- **The training room** (`?room=training`, `src/rendering/training-room.ts`): where the game now begins to move, after the owner judged the Hall too expensive to draw. A large, unremarkable, unfurnished meeting room on the ground floor of an English trading-estate office built in the 1990s, today, in the spirit of the original *The Office*, used as the training room for the Retro Encabulator. The company is clean and respectable and leaves nothing in a mess: keep it tidy. It meets present-day UK health and safety standards, and should go on meeting them: emergency lighting with a test key switch (BS 5266), a fire alarm call point at each exit (BS 5839), manifestation and a toughened-glass mark on the glazed fire exit, a vision panel reaching down to 500 mm on the door (Approved Document M), intumescent strips and smoke seals in its frame, the No Smoking sign the law requires, a first-aid notice and an electrical test label on the projector. The building's exterior is not modelled beyond what the windows show. Its plan is `src/game/training-room-plan.ts` (tested: a 14.4 × 9.6 m room on a 600 mm grid, every ceiling fitting and floor box in a tile of its own) and its build is `src/scene/training-room.ts`: magnolia walls, a suspended ceiling of speckled mineral tiles with recessed Category 2 fluorescent fittings, air-conditioning cassettes, smoke detectors, motion sensors and speakers; a hard vinyl floor on a raised access floor with flip-lid floor boxes (BS 1363 sockets and data), all shut; grey aluminium ribbon windows on the south and east walls with top-hung vents on restrictors, vertical blinds and low-surface-temperature radiators; a glazed fire exit with a panic bar in the east glazing under a running-man sign; on the internal walls, dado trunking with sockets, a clean whiteboard under a rolled-up projector screen, a pinboard, a clock, the fire alarm call point, sounder and FIRE ACTION notice, a hearing loop sign, an air-conditioning controller, a "please leave this room as you found it" notice and extinguishers on a stand; stacked chairs, folding tables on edge, a flip chart with a clean pad and a bin. Its door in the north wall (a beech fire door, built in `src/scene/portals.ts`) leads to dev0. Outside, under a grey overcast sky, is the Bracknell Road trading estate, and the view is nothing special: paving, shrub beds and a strip of mown lawn, the estate road with footways and street lights, and across it the other units, plain boxes with rows of windows, with car parks between them. Every parked car is at least 30 m from the windows, so their low polygon counts never show. Everything static is merged (`mergeStatic`), so it draws in about 140 calls and 14,000 triangles. `devTraining.view(room|windows|door|ceiling)`, `devTraining.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` and `devTraining.stats()` help in development; `?view=` takes the same names.

The development rooms:

- **dev0, Controls and Displays** (`/?room=dev0`, the default; `src/rendering/controls-room.ts`). The kinds of controls, displays and working parts the machine will be built from, taken from the motor control centre in the Retro Encabulator film (see `doc/research/retro-encabulator.md`). On the north wall, the CONTROLS board (`createControlsBoard` in `src/rendering/test-boards.ts`) holds one of each 30 mm pilot device, and the DISPLAYS board (`createDisplaysBoard`) holds one of each indicator and display with test controls under it. In the enlarged 20 × 16 m gallery, eighteen individual plinths exhibit every shared machine component (`src/rendering/machine-gallery.ts`, inventory and real product references in `src/game/machine-components.ts` and `src/game/machine-references.ts`); `?view=components` shows the gallery, and `?view=backplane` inspects the PCB BACKPLANE REV C assembly with five individually removable, distinctly named Pepperdyne expansion-card parts. Use picks up portable boards/cards at their pickup distance; the next Use drops them to fall, skitter and settle with dust and clatter. The shared handler is `src/rendering/object-handling.ts`, run by `first-person.ts` when a room supplies `objects`; boards own their effects in `src/rendering/portable-board.ts`. These include each working part of a motor control unit (`src/rendering/device-pedestals.ts`): the unit with its door, operating handle and breaker; a contactor; a thermal overload relay; a control fuse; a drive's power board with its bus capacitors; and an induction motor. Each pedestal has a sloping test panel that puts its part through what it does, failures included. `?view=controls`, `?view=displays` and `?view=pedestals` start in front of each. On the east wall, the CABINET FITTINGS board (`createCabinetBoard`, `?view=cabinet`) tries out the door fittings of the Retro Encabulator (dev6) before they are fitted to it, wired together as on a real unit: a 12-inch CRT monitor with four pictures (trend, mimic, alarm log, vectorscope), a strip-chart recorder, an alarm annunciator with TEST and ACK (`src/game/annunciator.ts`, the ISA flash-acknowledge sequence), a digital power monitor, a soft starter, a pushbutton station, a flange-mounted disconnect handle, louvres, a viewing window, and swatches of the worn pale-blue paint (`src/scene/worn-paint.ts`). The fittings are `src/scene/cabinet-fittings.ts`; new controls and displays are tried on a dev0 board first and then used on the machine, so improving one improves both. In development, `devControls.parts()` lists every clickable part, `devControls.work(name)` works one, `devControls.view(x, y, z, tx, ty, tz)` places the eye and `devControls.log()` reads the log.
- **dev1, Effects** (`/?room=dev1`, `src/rendering/effects-room.ts`): the VX3 Simulator's end-state effects room (colour channels, π drift, warp, sparks, smoke, embers), driven by `src/game/phase-effects.ts` and recorded with `src/game/effect-settings.ts`.
- **dev3, Racks** (`/?room=dev3`, `src/rendering/racks-room.ts`): the industrial design of 19-inch racks, built to catalogue dimensions (`doc/research/rack-dimensions.md`): a bayed suite of 42U server cabinets, an industrial enclosure on its plinth, a 24U cabinet on castors, an open two-post relay rack, a 12U cabinet and an open frame, each branded and filled with units of every kind, and loose units on display plinths with nameplates. It is mostly appearance: a click runs a unit out of its rack on its rails, or back (each unit is a `rackUnit` part). `src/scene/rack-equipment.ts` builds the cabinets (`createRackCabinet`, `createCabinetSuite`, `createRelayRack`) and the units (`createRackUnit`: blank, vented, handles, door, drawer, fans, drives, patch, shelf of half-width units, controls, display). `?view=racks` and `?view=plinths` start in front of each.
- **dev6, Retro Encabulator MCC** (`?room=dev6`, `src/rendering/encabulator-room.ts`): a seven-section Allen-Bradley CENTERLINE-style motor-control lineup, assembled from openable MCC bucket doors and the existing industrial controls, device views and rack units (`src/scene/retro-encabulator-mcc.ts`). It uses 90-inch sections, six-inch wireways and 6.5-inch bucket increments. A wider PLC bay contains period 1771-style I/O and reused rack units; starters expose breakers, contactors, overloads, fuses and terminals. The development room links west to dev3 and east to dev7. Every unit's door has its own face, after the film's line-up and with some licence (`dressDoor`): CRT monitors, a strip-chart recorder, an annunciator, a PanelView-style operator terminal behind a viewing window onto the PLC rack, a drive keypad, a soft starter, power monitors, meters and gauges, LED bar graphs and dot matrices, pushbutton stations, flange handles, louvres, a message display over the control section and a stack light on top; its displays move. The supply comes in through the incoming supply isolator (`src/scene/incoming-isolator.ts`), an enclosed rotary main switch, a yellow box with a red pistol-grip handle turned a quarter turn from 0 to I, after Eaton's P3-100/I5/SVB (100 A, three poles, 200 × 280 × 169 mm, under the maker's name VOLTARA), on a galvanised unistrut stand to the left of the line-up, fed from overhead in rigid conduit into a gland in its top and feeding the first section through a conduit from its side; it starts off. The line-up is re-rated to match: MAIN DISCONNECT is 100 A. MAIN INCOMING, where the supply lands, is before the main disconnect: live whenever the isolator is on, its three voltmeters (L1-L2, L2-L3, L3-L1) then reading the nominal 480 V whatever the main disconnect is doing, and silent, being only lugs and meters. The main disconnect feeds everything else, so the machine needs both on (`mains`). MAIN INCOMING and MAIN DISCONNECT do not open: they are flat covers screwed on at their corners (`fixed` on a bucket), their fittings on the cover, their works inside never seen; not every unit opens. While the machine has power it sounds, quietly, from where each sound comes from: the drive train's motor hum, and the control CRT's and the PLC chassis's fans, the drive's heatsink fan, the hydrocoptic pump and the panendermic blower, each a machine tone (dev12) held from inside its bucket (`tone` on a bucket in `BAYS`), running up as the breaker closes and down as it opens; the other buckets are silent. No held sound anywhere is a bare synthesised tone any more: the parts' mains hum, contactor hold and motor hum (`part-loops.ts`) are machine tones too. Each screen's power-on self-test runs at its own pace (`bootPace` in `src/game/screen-boot.ts`, tested): its boot takes 0.8 to 1.25 times 15 seconds, each new screen stepping on by the golden ratio so no two finish within half a second of each other, and the gaps between its messages are uneven, from its name. Each door has a flat face with a deeper pan behind its middle and a ventilated rear cover; clicking its back closes it through the same door component, while only its two black wing latches on the free edge open it; everything else on it works as a control (buttons dip, disconnect handles throw, selectors and knobs turn, the mushroom stop latches, the annunciator's TEST and ACK work), so a click meant for a button never opens a door. The machine can fail like an engine giving up, after the Millennium Falcon's hyperdrive in *The Empire Strikes Back* (`src/game/machine-failure.ts`, tested): every running sound follows one machine speed (`pitch`, set by a scenario, times a failure's strain), so a failure bends the real hum, with a thin spooling whine sliding down over it, coughs and flickering lamps. HICCUP sags and trips some sounds out for a moment, then carries on; SPUTTER falters, catches, fails again and stalls (powered but stopped and silent); RESTART, also from the blue RESET on the front of Automation Control when stalled, coughs it back into life; KAPUT, the most dramatic, winds up hopefully, gives way with bangs and sparks from the drive and Automation Control, retries desperately and dies, tripping the main breaker. A FAILURE SIMULATOR lectern in front of the line-up (`src/rendering/failure-console.ts`) has a button for each and a RESET (`mcc.recover`). `devEncabulator.view(...)`, `devEncabulator.door(n)` and `devEncabulator.fail(id)`, `restart()`, `recover()` and `failure()` help in development. The steel is a worn modernist pale blue (`MCC_BLUE`): grime at the edges and feet, hand marks by the handles, chips and scratches, and a base plinth (`MCC_PLINTH`) heavily scuffed by boots and trolleys. Bucket contents render and take clicks only while the corresponding door is physically open, including its opening and closing animation; closed interiors are culled as whole groups. `?view=lineup|controls|plc` supplies inspection starts; `?view=backs` is a rear-cover inspection camera; doors start closed unless an explicit `?doors=open` preview is requested.
- **dev7, Corridor** (`?room=dev7`, `src/rendering/corridor-room.ts`): a zig-zagging basement passage beneath the plant with branches to a locker room, service bay, plant room and lift lobby. The reference photographs are copied into `resources/reference/underground-corridors/`. `src/game/corridor-plan.ts` defines the connected walkable footprint and collision walls; `src/scene/underground-corridor.ts` models painted blockwork, concrete floors, fluorescent fixtures, overhead services, fire doors, signs and connected industrial spaces. The room links west to dev6 and east to dev8. `?view=entry|dogleg|junction|lockers|lift` supplies inspection starts.
- **dev8, Building Fittings** (`?room=dev8`, `src/rendering/fittings-room.ts`): the fittings of a present-day English office building, each a working part (`src/rendering/building-parts.ts`, drawn by `src/scene/building-fittings.ts`), all bound to the room's own world model of its building services (`src/game/building-services.ts`, tested), so they do what they would in a real building; new building fittings are tried here first and then used in the training room, as dev0 does for the machine. Each development room may have a world model of its own like this one. North wall, LIGHTING: two-way switching (a switch here and one at the far end, on the south wall), a dimmer, the emergency lighting test key switch, a maintained exit sign, an occupancy sensor that turns the lights off after 90 s without movement, and non-maintained emergency lights that light when the lighting circuit fails or is tested; ACCESSIBLE WC: a pull-cord light, an extractor with run-on, and the emergency pull cord, reset button and over-door light. In the middle of the room, the dev room's joke: a steel waste-paper bin under the smoke detector, and beside it a button post copied from catalogue parts (a galvanised steel post on a bolted base plate carrying a Schneider XALD01-style single-hole control station, 68 × 68 × 53 mm, light grey with a dark grey lid), facing away from the bin so whoever presses its LIGHT THE BIN button, a red illuminated push button lit while the bin burns, is looking at the bin, under a yellow DO NOT PRESS THIS BUTTON sign clamped to the post; it sets off the whole sequence of a two-stage fire alarm with a voice alarm, as in a UK office (BS 5839-1 and -8). Lit, the bin smoulders for a few seconds, smoking a little, then flames catch and grow (particle flames, yellow at the root, orange and red as they rise, drawn additively), and their smoke, rising in a plume and spreading under the ceiling, sets off the detector; in the alert stage the fire doors' holders release and the voice alarm (a ceiling speaker) sounds the alert tone and asks everyone to stay where they are while it is investigated, for 90 seconds, the panel's output delays lamp flashing. The investigation finds, at random, a fire or a false alarm; a false alarm is announced while the bin still burns, so its smoke soon sets the detector off again and the second activation goes straight to evacuation. In evacuation the voice alarm plays the evacuation signal (the VX3 Simulator's synthesised alarm, `src/audio/fire-alarm.ts`) and the evacuation message, the access door unlocks, the brigade is called (the panel's remote output lamp), and the fire grows until the hot gas at the sprinkler head over the bin reaches 68 °C, about 18 seconds in: its glass bulb bursts, it sprays, the sprinkler alarm bell rings, and the water puts the bin out within a quarter of a minute. A call point, or water flowing, goes straight to evacuation. The messages are recordings of a calm British male voice (`src/audio/assets/voice-alarm/`, credited in `CREDITS.md`). The panel is a two-zone conventional panel modelled on a common British one (C-TEC's CFP: an off-white case, a charcoal facia with printed instructions, numbered keys, colour-coded white, red and green buttons, a red key standing out of a keyhole in the facia, and fire, zone and general lamps), under a maker's name of our own: its red button silences or resounds, its green resets (only once nothing is still in alarm, then the all-clear is announced), both only with the key turned; its white silences its own buzzer and key 4 tests its lamps. At the valve set the main valve stops the water and RESET replaces burst heads. On the east wall, FIRE: the panel, a resettable call point, sounder-beacons, water, foam, CO₂ and powder extinguishers (a click pulls the pin, the next discharges it), and a fire door on a magnetic holder. South wall, POWER and CLIMATE: a distribution board (main switch and lighting, sockets and HVAC breakers), a twin socket with testers plugged in, a fused spur, a floor box, the air-conditioning controller and its ceiling cassette, a dial thermostat, a radiator with its thermostatic valve and a window with a vent and blinds; the room's temperature follows from them all. West wall, ACCESS: a maglocked door released by a card reader, push-to-exit or the green emergency door release (reset by its key), which also unlocks in a fire, and an intercom. The room's own lights are the model's. `devFittings.model` is the model, `devFittings.view(bin|overview|lighting|fire|power|access)` (the room starts at `bin`, looking past the pedestal at the bin) and `devFittings.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` place the eye; `?view=` takes the same names.
- **dev9, The Wandering Button** (`?room=dev9`, `src/rendering/wandering-room.ts`): a red illuminated push button (the XB4-style part) in an XALD-style control station on a mobile floor stand (a weighted steel base on four swivel castors that turn to the way it rolls and rumble on the floor, and a galvanised post with a gland-fitted cable loop entering a rubber grommet, and mounting bolts; `createMobileStand` in `src/scene/building-fittings.ts`), and on the north wall a 300 × 300 × 200 mm steel mode panel after PanelSeT CRN, with one four-position cam MODE selector (NORMAL, EAGER, SKITTISH, TERRIFIED; after Harmony K1D004NLH), four labelled XB4 active-mode pilot lights beneath it and a separate amber PRESSED lamp, all devices at true size. Its behaviour is tested pure logic in `src/game/mobile-pedestal.ts`, with the room's small world model: NORMAL, it stays in the middle of the room (going back there at a creep); EAGER, it creeps at a quarter of walking speed to the spot on the floor that puts its red button nearest the middle of your view, no nearer than pressing distance and within the walls; when that sight-line target is unreachable, it approaches until its lens is 0.5 m away, continuously turning to face the player (or uses the nearest clear spot if a wall blocks that space). It waits a random 1–4 seconds of still camera (ignoring tiny jitter), then turns its red face square-on to the viewing axis and travels at the same time, both easing up and down with maximum speeds of 1.5 rad/s and 0.5 m/s; SKITTISH, it plays the faster man in the "lion and man" pursuit game: it keeps to a circle round the middle of the room (0.6 m off the nearer walls), and when you come within 2.5 m it runs round it away from you, reversing whenever you cut ahead, at 1.8 times your speed, with its button turned away, so it can never be caught (it never runs into a corner, where it would be); solving the chase shows a chaser comes within 1.2 m only below about 1.67 times, and at 1.8 the best chaser comes no nearer than 1.42 m (`doc/research/skittish-chase.md`); TERRIFIED, it first heads to the furthest corner and then, when approached there, escapes to another randomly chosen corner, avoiding the player on the way. During the last two metres of each corner journey it turns to face the room centre and keeps that orientation while waiting. After three such escapes (the initial trip does not count) it stops to be pressed; pressing it produces a brief spray of sparks and returns its mode, selector and active lamp to NORMAL, dormant where it stands until another mode is selected. The red button lamp is driven independently of presses: off in NORMAL, a double-pulse heartbeat at 60 bpm in EAGER, 90 bpm in SKITTISH and 120 bpm in TERRIFIED. The separate PRESSED wall lamp still records presses for three seconds. Its castors play a credited, looped shopping-cart recording while it moves or turns. You cannot walk through it, nor it through you. `devWandering.model`, `devWandering.stand()`, `devWandering.view(room|knob|button)`, `devWandering.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)`, `devWandering.probe(x, y)` (what a click at that screen point hits, and whether it works it) and `devWandering.use(x, y)` help in development.
- **dev10, Waking Up from the Dream** (`?room=dev10`, `src/rendering/waking-room.ts`): each waking is into a different room, and the room before was a dream. A use (a click, E, a tap or A) works nothing here: it wakes the player (a click on a portal still travels). The eyes snap shut; while they are shut the walls are papered afresh and the camera is moved to one of six places in the room, looking diagonally down, so neither change is seen; the eyes open slowly onto a dim, blurred view and blink dizzily, three times, each opening wider and clearer, while the head shakes itself awake: a slow groggy sway from the moment the eyes first open, with a quick side-to-side shake and roll at each blink and the hardest once they are open, turned about the top of the spine (12 cm below and 9 cm behind the eyes) so the eyes swing as well as turn (left out under reduced motion); about six seconds after the use, the player has the camera back; another use wakes again. The walls are garish abstract patterns of the early 1990s, our own designs in their spirit, drawn on seamless canvas tiles 1.6 m across from a seed, so no two rooms are alike (`src/scene/nineties-patterns.ts`): teal brush swathes crossed by purple squiggles, as on the paper cup, Memphis confetti, arcade carpet, shards, neon splatter, checks under pastel squiggles, windbreaker colour blocks and confetti chips. `src/game/dream-rooms.ts` (tested) deals them like a shuffled deck, every pattern once before any again, never the same pattern or place twice running. The pattern is on the walls themselves and runs under the portals, which (in every room) stand 2 mm off the wall, drawn over it by polygon offset. The blink is subjective, one view closing as the two eyes are seen as one: a broad, out-of-focus upper lid comes down across the whole view, the lower rising a little to meet it, the light dimming as they close, the dull red-brown of light through skin when shut (`src/rendering/eyelid-overlay.ts`, drawn over the rendered frame). The timeline is tested pure logic in `src/game/waking.ts` (the camera moves only while the eyes are shut, at any frame rate). While it plays the room holds the player with `hold` in `first-person.ts`, which ignores walking and looking from every input and binds nothing. Entering the room again starts the dream again, with plain walls. A 16 × 12 m room with portals to dev9 and dev11. `devWaking.wake()`, `devWaking.dream()`, `devWaking.at(t)` (hold the waking at `t` seconds, `null` to let it run), `devWaking.paper(pattern, seed)`, `devWaking.room()`, `devWaking.frame()`, `devWaking.view(room)` and `devWaking.place(x, z, yaw, pitch)` help in development.
- **dev11, The Button Gallery** (`?room=dev11`, `src/rendering/button-gallery-room.ts`): the wandering button's stand (dev9) in every state of repair, seven in a row across a dim gallery, each under its own spotlight with a museum label on a low lectern: 1 NEW, chrome-plated all over (brushed, strongly reflecting the studio) in a breathing glow, its lamp lit; 2 IN SERVICE, as in dev9; 3 WORN (scratches, grime, a hairline crack, a scuffed lens); 4 KNOCKED ABOUT (the post bent, a cracked lens, more cracks); 5 VANDALISED (graffiti tags and a sticker, rust, a crazed lens); 6 SET ON FIRE (soot, heat tint, the case charred and slumped, the cable melted, a chipped lens); 7 WRECKED (the post torn most of the way through and hanging at 68°, its wiring pulled out, a castor gone, the lens shattered, its lamp flickering, white through the hole when lit and grey plastic when not, and whatever vital fluid it runs on mostly leaked out: brown runs down the post, a pool round its foot running over the edge of the base, and a glossy puddle on the floor pulled out to the side its head hangs over, which mirrors what is bright above it through a planar reflection masked to its shape, three's `Reflector` with a shader of our own). The conditions are tested pure data in `src/game/stand-conditions.ts`, each keeping every hurt of the one before and adding more; `src/scene/stand-condition.ts` draws them, the wear painted on canvases from a seed. `createMobileStand` (in `building-fittings.ts`) now takes `chrome` and carries everything from its bend point (`STAND_BEND_Y`, 0.55 m) up in `head`, so a post can bend there; dev9's stand is unchanged. The buttons press. The lamps of those that still work (1 to 4) are lit and gleam, with a red halo and a little red light cast on the box (`createLampGlow` in `src/scene/button-effects.ts`); the new one gives off, seen up close, tiny motes of red light rising off its lens, as if power were here (`createPowerMotes`); from 5 on, each button shorts out now and then, a blue-white flash at the edge of its lens, a few small sparks, its lamp jumping on and a quiet crackle (`createShortSpark`), at random gaps with a median of 9 seconds and never under 1.5 (`src/game/shorting.ts`, tested). ## Architecture

- `src/game/` is pure logic with no DOM or Three.js, with its tests beside it (`*.test.ts`).
- **Components** (`src/game/world/components.ts`) are every part's behaviour, apart from any model of the machine: inputs (Power, Latch), one source of truth for state (a bound world value or its own), guarded operator actions, silent sets from the model, and the events they make (press, on, off, detent, trip, pull-in, blow and the rest), which their part sounds. Operator controls: LED pushbutton, momentary, switch, selector, key-locked selector, operating handle, mushroom stop, push-to-test pilot light. **Self-acting** parts move by what flows through them and sound as they act, whatever moved them: contactor (pull-in, drop-out, welded contacts), thermal overload relay (trip class, RESET once cool, TEST), fuse (I²t, replace), electrolytic capacitor (bulge, vent, smoke, replace), and the hour meter. Use a component for everything a player can work or a failure can strike; failures of board-level parts (a blown capacitor) are components too.
- **Views** (`src/scene/`) only draw. `industrial-controls.ts`: 30 mm push buttons, mushroom head, lever and key selectors, potentiometer, pilot lamp, legend plates and nameplates. `industrial-displays.ts`: stack light, hour meter, message display, operator terminal, drive keypad. `industrial-devices.ts`: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse holder, capacitor board and motor. The 1980s parts from the VX3 Simulator (`eighties-controls.ts`, `panel-switchgear.ts`, `panel-instruments.ts`) are kept and reused.
- **Parts** (`src/rendering/parts.ts`) are the controls and devices as whole things, like React components: each is made from its props alone (colours, legend, what it does, and its bindings to the world: `bound`, `power`, `latch`) and brings its look (the views), its behaviour (the component), what a click on it does and its own sounds, defined beside it as recordings or synthesised clicks and held loops (`src/audio/part-loops.ts`). Bound, a part shows and writes a world value; unbound, it keeps its own, so an LED pushbutton wired to nothing still toggles. Parts play through one shared, positional audio output (`src/audio/part-audio.ts`), which starts at the first click or key press; a room only calls `partAudio.listen(camera, metre)` each frame. A room adds its parts to a `PartSet` for their clicks and updates and never wires a sound or a click by hand; there is no table of sounds anywhere. There are parts for push buttons, LED pushbuttons, pilot lights, lever and key selectors, potentiometers, the emergency stop, disconnect handles, key switches, pushbutton stations, the drive keypad and operator terminal, door latches, rack-mount units (a click runs one out of its rack; a drawer rattles), and the devices: the motor control unit, contactor, overload relay, fuse, capacitor board and motor. dev0, dev3 and dev6 are built from them; the building fittings in `src/rendering/building-parts.ts` (dev8) are built the same way, on `beginPart`.
- **The world graph** (`src/game/world/graph.ts`) is a directed graph of cached functions on signals: inputs, fixed parameters, states, derived values and outputs, with declared actions and processes, and `analyse()` to list dead controls and unseen values. The machine will be built on it. How state divides between the world, derived physics and presentation, and how parts bind to it, is in `doc/design/world-model.md`.
- Printed lettering (`createMicroLabel`) never takes a click: its sheet is larger than its letters, and it must not block the control it labels. A tap works what the finger first touched, and a touch must move 12 px (a mouse 5) before it counts as a drag.
- Every knob and rotary selector turns the same way: a click on its left half turns it anticlockwise (down), its right half clockwise (up), one detent per click (`knobDirection` in `src/scene/rotary-knob.ts`).
- **Controls** (`src/game/controls.ts`, `src/rendering/first-person.ts`): one scheme for every room. Keyboard and mouse: WASD or arrows walk, Shift runs, a click captures the mouse to look, a click or E uses, a click on a knob's left or right half turns it down or up, the wheel (or a trackpad pinch) zooms, C and V crouch, Alt+wheel sets the eye height, Esc frees the cursor. Touch: a left thumbstick walks (pushed to the rim, runs), a right thumbstick or a drag looks, a tap uses (a knob's left or right half turns it), a two-finger pinch zooms, and a CROUCH button sits above the left stick. Gamepad (standard mapping): the left stick walks (click it to run), the right stick looks, A uses, LB and RB turn a knob down and up (`turningKnobs` in `src/scene/rotary-knob.ts`), the triggers zoom, B crouches. The controls follow the last input used, and a help line at the foot of the screen (`controlsHelp`) shows that input's controls.
- Model fittings on real catalogue parts, at true size: find the product in a manufacturer's catalogue or datasheet (Schneider Electric's are good) and copy its dimensions and look, under a maker's name of our own. Always use the correct-sized component, and never scale a part to fit: a small control station (Schneider XALD) takes 22 mm devices, so it gets the XB4 series (`series: 'xb4'` on the selector and pilot light parts, `createXB4Selector` and `createXB4PilotLight` in `src/scene/industrial-controls.ts`), not the 30 mm oiltight devices shrunk. The illuminated push button (`createPushButton`, head `illuminated`) copies Schneider's Harmony XB4 (XB4BW34M5): a chromed bezel about 30 mm across, 13.5 mm deep, round a flush, faintly domed lens lit from within; the fire alarm panel copies C-TEC's CFP.
- `src/rendering/first-person.ts` walks, looks and zooms. The game aims for realism: the VX3's PS1 look is retired. `src/rendering/ps1-material.ts` keeps its old name but now only makes the realistic standard material for the WebGL rooms (dev0 to dev3), which are still to move to WebGPURenderer like the Hall.
- Mains in the development rooms is American: 60 Hz, 480 V three-phase power, 120 V control. In the training room it is British: 230 V, 50 Hz, BS 1363 sockets.
- Everything is in English: labels, legends, log lines and notes. Some text carried over from the VX3 Simulator may still be German; translate it when found.
