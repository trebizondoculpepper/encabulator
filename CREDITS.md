## Procedural effects

Control clicks, hums, dropped-board clatters and other synthesised sounds are original procedural patches in `src/audio/`. They use Web Audio and [ZzFX](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel/ZzFX), by [Frank Force](https://github.com/KilledByAPixel), MIT.

The fire alarm sounder (`src/toolkit/components/fire/audio/fire-alarm.ts`), copied from the VX3 Simulator, is synthesised from scratch. Its arrangement was taken, by measurement only, from [Hausalarm Auslösen German fire alarm](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B7Mqzvsw7iU) by [USfireFan](https://www.youtube.com/@USfireFan), supplied by the owner: a ~3.1 kHz piezo pulsing half a second on and off, a two-note buzzer in its gaps, and a far sounder sweeping down from ~3.25 to ~2.1 kHz. Pitches and timings were read from a spectrogram; no audio from the video is included or played, and our pitches differ.

## Procedural circuit boards

The cabinet backplane trace generator (`src/scene/vendor/circuit-traces.ts`) is adapted from [Circuit Board Painter](https://github.com/ArtBIT/circuitboard-painter) by ArtBIT, copyright 2025, under the MIT licence. The full notice is retained in `src/scene/vendor/circuitboard-painter.LICENSE`. Adaptations remove the editor and flow-field dependency, use a local seeded RNG, reserve board-edge clearance and draw static solder-mask, via and silkscreen textures for Three.js. Backplane geometry and assembly markings are original project work.

The pre-rendered green PCB textures in `src/toolkit/components/industrial-control/assets/pcb/` are generated from that same MIT-licensed trace algorithm by `scripts/bake-pcb-textures.ts`, using [@napi-rs/canvas](https://github.com/Brooooooklyn/canvas) (MIT) as a development-time renderer. The seeds and dimensions are retained in the texture manifest; no reference photographs are included in the game.

The Encabulator drive-train hum (`src/audio/machine-hum.ts`) is original project synthesis, written with OpenAI Codex: three speed-controlled sine partials, with no third-party recording or sample; distributed under the project's licence. [Source](src/audio/machine-hum.ts).

Before the owner's take, the Level 2 main-power instructions were rendered from [Hexgrad's Kokoro-82M](https://huggingface.co/hexgrad/Kokoro-82M), `bm_george`, under Apache-2.0. Edits: 0.92 speaking speed, 75 Hz high-pass, -18 LUFS normalisation and mono 24 kHz / 64 kb/s MP3 encoding, matching the existing narrator placeholders.

The Level 2 narrator has been replaced by the owner's own performance (Sal Fadhley, the game's author, who reserves all rights), recorded in one continuous take of about 31 minutes on a wireless lavalier microphone (48 kHz, 32-bit float, mono). The take is transcribed with Whisper large-v3, each speech is matched to its clip by content, and cut at real pauses (silence below -50 dB lasting at least 0.65 s), with 0.25 s of room tone before and 0.4 s after and 12 ms fades; the whole take is raised by one gain of +4 dB (it measures -22.2 LUFS), high-passed at 75 Hz, limited and encoded as mono 24 kHz / 64 kb/s MP3 under each clip's existing asset key. Every clip is now the owner's except `cue_090` ("Ahem", not performed), which is a beat of the take's room tone, and `startup_main_reset`, which the owner did perform and is also replaced. The "x down, y to go" count variants were not recorded and are no longer used. The scripts are in `resources/sound/narrations/level2/owner-take/`; the raw take and its transcripts stay out of the repository.

The October 2026 throw-switch thocks in `src/toolkit/components/core/parts/parts.ts` and `src/toolkit/components/industrial-control/parts/machine-components.ts` are original project synthesis: lower filtered noise, short 105–145 Hz body impacts and reduced metallic ringing. No new recording or sample was used.

The development-only comparison loads the original `resources/models/bbc-micro/virtualbeeb/beeb.glb`, unchanged on disk, with its own keycaps; runtime edits only: opaque material conversion, static mesh merging by material and an inset curved live CRT. The decimated derivative `beeb-low.glb` (glTF Transform 4.5.1 simplification, which collapsed the keys) is no longer loaded; it retains Ant Mercer's attribution and the source restrictions above. The procedural baseline keyboard keeps shaped, lettered caps, merged by colour.

The procedural four-way UK floor extension at the BBC workstation is modelled to the 321 × 60 × 33 mm dimensions of the [Masterplug EXS1342W-01](https://www.masterplug.com/uk/products/work/heavy-duty-extension-leads/EXS1342W-01). Geometry, plugs, power cords and monitor signal cable are original project work; no manufacturer mesh or photograph is included.

The workstation's later floor-bolted power box reuses the existing procedural UK metalclad twin-socket model, with an original mounting plate, four anchor bolts and two plugs. It replaces the earlier Masterplug-style loose extension. No additional third-party mesh or texture is used.

## Procedural cabinet rear fittings

The cabinet rear panels, service leaves, glands/blanking plates, protective bonding leads, covered duct and fan/filter are original project geometry. Construction/dimensional references: [Rockwell CENTERLINE 2100 selection guide](https://literature.rockwellautomation.com/idc/groups/literature/documents/sg/2100-sg003_-en-p.pdf), [Rittal VX25 rear-panel construction](https://www.rittal.com/imf/none/3_11523/8685000_Instructions_DE_EN_FR), [Rittal protective bonding guidance](https://www.rittal.com/imf/none/3_6978/8685000_Protective_conductor_brochure_EN), [Phoenix Contact CD 60X40, 3240190](https://www.phoenixcontact.com/de-lu/produkte/verdrahtungskanal-cd-60x40-3240190), [Rittal SK 3237.100 fan/filter dimensions](https://www.rittal.com/pdf-creator/variant/lt-en/3237100), and [icotek KEL-U cable-entry construction](https://www.icotek.com/en/products/cable-entry-systems/kel-u-42250). Reference documents/photos remain research material; no manufacturer mesh, photo or texture is rendered in the game. The rear-access arrangement is fictional Neumann Dynamik engineering fitted to the existing seven-section machine, rather than a claimed catalogue CENTERLINE option. Door sounds reuse the existing credited recordings/synthesis.

The procedural rubber floor cable protector uses the [D-Line FC83 floor-cover profile](https://www.d-line-it.com/cable-protectors/) as its dimensional reference: 83 mm overall width, 14 mm height and a 30 × 10 mm underside channel ([manufacturer brochure](https://www.d-line-it.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/04/brochure_uk_2020.pdf)). The curved sweep, rubber material, cable and plugs are original project geometry; no manufacturer mesh or photograph is included. The BBC data lead uses its rear RS423 5-pin DIN interface, documented in [R. T. Russell's BBC transfer instructions](https://www.bbcbasic.co.uk/bbcwin/manual/bbcwinf.html). The cabinet end is a fictional low-voltage data gland in the existing rear entry plate, not a new mains connection.

## Neumann diagnostic terminal and controller

The restrained floppy-head seek sequence (paired filtered-noise steps with 130 Hz body taps) and Link Controller connection beep (880 Hz, 90 ms) are original project synthesis in `bbc-workstation.ts` and `neumann-diagnostics.ts`. Author: Encabulator project contributors; project licence; no external recording or sample. Existing credited BBC startup recording is unchanged. The new diagnostic motherboard, board faces and chips are original procedural geometry; daughterboard dimensions follow the 100 × 160 mm single-Euroboard format documented by [nVent SCHROFF’s guide-rail specification](https://www.nvent.com/en-us/schroff/products/enc64560-074). The Neumann-specific circuitry and mounting arrangement are fictional, not a manufacturer product claim. No manufacturer mesh or image is included.

## BBC Micro resynthesized melodies

Reference: [“BBC Micro — Sound Chip”](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KgPFSxlXM6o), uploaded by **Fizgog**, 3 February 2023. The uploader describes direct SN76489 playback and identifies the underlying effects as **Pitfall**; composition authorship has not been independently verified. **Source licence: not stated in retrieved metadata; reuse permission unconfirmed.** Four selected approximate transcriptions are generated with new band-limited square waves, integer-divider pitches for a 4 MHz clock and speaker roll-off. They use no audio samples from the recording. Their original mono case-resonance processing uses 80% of the earlier wet contribution. Synthesis/processing implementation: Encabulator project contributors, original project code; melody provenance remains as above. Fanfare trim: source 3.80–4.82 s, verified spectrally. The original download remains a local provenance archive; obsolete cleaned/dry audition files were deleted at the owner’s request. The selected fanfare and beware cues are prepared as `src/toolkit/components/sound/audio/assets/bbc-fanfare.mp3` and `bbc-beware.mp3`; all four selected cues and the reproducible script are in `resources/sound/effects/bbc-micro-melodies/`. Hardware reference: [Texas Instruments SN76489AN data sheet](https://map.grauw.nl/resources/sound/texas_instruments_sn76489an.pdf).
